Statutory Foundation

Why Formal Copyright Registration Matters

While copyright subsists automatically upon creation, proving exact date of origination and true authorship in a dispute without a government certificate is notoriously arduous. Infringers frequently counter-claim independent creation or fabricate earlier dates.

Under Section 48 of the Copyright Act, 1957, the Register of Copyrights maintained by the Central Government serves as prima facie evidence of the particulars entered therein. Presenting an official Certificate of Registration dramatically strengthens court applications for interim injunctions, asset seizures, and domain takedowns.

Documentation Checklist

Required Documents for Form XIV Filing

Accurate statutory schedules ensure smooth progression through the mandatory 30-day scrutiny window.

Mandatory Filing Records

  • Copies of the Work: 2 complete physical copies or digital uploads of the manuscript, artistic work, or audiovisual production.
  • Software Code Extracts: For software/code, printouts of the first and last 10–20 pages of source code with confidential portions redacted.
  • Form XIV & Statement of Particulars: Fully drafted particulars of authorship, publication year, and language.
  • Power of Attorney (PoA): Executed advocate authorization on non-judicial stamp paper.

Consents & Clearance Records

  • Author No-Objection Certificate (NOC): Mandatory if the applicant is a company or employer and not the individual natural author.
  • Publisher NOC: Required if the work has already been commercially published by a third-party publishing house.
  • Trademark Clearance (TM-C / Search Certificate): Mandatory under Rule 70(6) if the artistic work is capable of being used in relation to any goods or services.
  • Certificate of Incorporation: For corporate and startup applicants.
Procedural Roadmap

The Copyright Registration Process

How our intellectual property team navigates the Copyright Office timeline.

STEP 01

Classification & Audit

Verifying originality, determining appropriate work classification (Literary, Artistic, Software), and auditing chain of title.

STEP 03

30-Day Mandatory Wait

STEP 04

Examiner Scrutiny

Formal examination by the Copyright Office examiner. If discrepancy letters issue, our advocates prepare comprehensive legal replies.

STEP 05

Hearing (If Required)

If third-party objections or statutory discrepancies persist, advocates appear before the Registrar to present oral and documentary proof.

STEP 06

Certificate Issuance

Formal Registration Certificate is issued under the seal of the Copyright Office, and details are entered into the Register of Copyrights.

Statutory Mandate

Statutory Protections Under the Copyright Act, 1957

The Copyright Act grants authors an exclusive bundle of economic and moral rights under Section 14, including the sole authority to reproduce, issue copies, perform in public, make translations, and adapt the protected work.

  • Section 51 (Infringement): Defines actionable infringement when any person without license does anything the exclusive right to do which is conferred upon the owner.
  • Section 57 (Moral Rights): Grants the author special moral rights to claim authorship and restrain distortion/mutilation even after complete economic assignment.
  • Section 63 (Penalties): Knowing infringement is punishable with imprisonment for not less than 6 months up to 3 years and a fine of ₹50,000 to ₹2,00,000.

Software Copyright vs Patent

In India, Section 3(k) of the Patents Act, 1970 strictly excludes "a computer programme per se" from patentability unless tied to novel hardware utility.

Consequently, Copyright Registration serves as the primary statutory shield for software technology companies, SaaS ventures, and mobile application architectures in India. It protects the actual expressive code, structural modules, and user interfaces from competitive copying.

Important Notice: Copyright protects original expression, not abstract ideas, procedures, or mathematical methods.
