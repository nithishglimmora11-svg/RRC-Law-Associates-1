NRI Income Tax Return Filing Services in India

Demystifying Indian direct tax obligations for Non-Resident Indians (NRIs), Overseas Citizens of India (OCIs), and persons of Indian origin residing abroad.

For Indians living abroad, maintaining regulatory compliance with the Indian Income Tax Department is vital to protect domestic assets, repatriate funds smoothly, and prevent adverse tax notices.

NRI income tax return filing is the formal process of declaring income earned, accrued, or received within India during a financial year (1 April to 31 March). Unlike resident citizens whose worldwide income is subject to Indian tax, Non-Resident Indians are taxed solely on their Indian-source income under Section 5(2) of the Income-tax Act.

Your residential status under Section 6 is determined by the physical count of days spent in India during the relevant financial year. Whether classified as a Non-Resident (NR) or Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR), this status directly governs the taxability of your earnings, eligible deductions, and withholding tax obligations.

Scope of Taxable Indian Income: Rental proceeds from Indian properties, interest earned on Non-Resident Ordinary (NRO) accounts, capital gains from the sale of Indian real estate or equities, and professional fees received for services rendered in India.

Navigating Indian tax filing from abroad can be daunting due to rigorous withholding taxes (often up to 30% plus surcharges under Section 195), automated cross-verification through the Annual Information Statement (AIS), and complex Double Taxation Avoidance Agreements (DTAA). RRC Law Associates provides strategic legal oversight alongside qualified tax professionals to ensure your filings are precise, compliant, and optimized against double taxation.

Legal & Regulatory Framework

Income Tax Considerations for NRIs

Essential statutory nuances governing non-resident tax liability under the Income-tax Act, 1961, and preparing for the transitional framework of the Income-tax Act, 2025.

Residential Status Determination

Tax liability in India begins with Section 6 of the Income-tax Act. An individual is an NRI if they spend fewer than 182 days in India during the financial year (or fewer than 60 days if visiting India, relaxed to 182 days for Indian citizens or persons of Indian origin on a visit).

For individuals with Indian-source income exceeding ₹15 Lakhs (excluding foreign income), the stay threshold is narrowed to 120 days, resulting in Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR) status.

Governed by Section 6(1) & 6(1A)

Scope of Indian-Source Income

Under Section 5(2), an NRI is liable to Indian income tax only on income that is received or deemed to be received in India, or income that accrues or arises (or is deemed to accrue or arise) in India under Section 9.

Income earned abroad from employment, business, or foreign assets is entirely exempt from Indian taxation and does not need to be declared in an NRI tax return.

Governed by Section 5(2) & Section 9

Tax on Indian Rental Income

Rent earned from leasing Indian real estate is taxed under "Income from House Property". NRIs can claim a standard deduction of 30% on Net Annual Value under Section 24(a) for repairs, irrespective of actual expenses.

Municipal taxes paid and interest paid on housing loans under Section 24(b) are deductible. Tenants paying rent to NRIs must deduct TDS at 31.2% under Section 195.

Governed by Sections 22 to 24 & 195

Taxation of NRE vs NRO Interest

Interest earned on Non-Resident External (NRE) accounts and Foreign Currency Non-Resident (FCNR) deposits is completely exempt from Indian income tax under Section 10(4)(ii).

Conversely, interest on Non-Resident Ordinary (NRO) accounts is fully taxable in India and subject to mandatory withholding tax at 30% plus applicable education cess (31.2%).

Governed by Section 10(4)(ii) & Section 195

Capital Gains on Real Estate Sales

Selling immovable property in India generates Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) if held over 24 months. The buyer is statutorily mandated to withhold TDS at 20% (plus surcharge and cess) on the total sale value unless a Lower TDS Certificate is secured.

Tax liability can be legally mitigated by reinvesting capital gains in another residential property in India (Section 54) or specified capital gains bonds (Section 54EC).

Governed by Sections 45, 54, 54EC & 195

DTAA Relief & Statutory Transition

Under Section 90, NRIs can opt for provisions of the Indian Income-tax Act or the bilateral Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA), whichever is more beneficial, by furnishing a valid Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) and Form 10F.

Statutory Note on Direct Tax Reform: The Indian direct tax framework is anchored in the Income-tax Act, 1961, with enacted modernisation under the Income-tax Act, 2025 scheduled for rollout from 1 April 2026.

Governed by Sections 90 & 91 / Direct Tax Code
Preparation Checklist

Documents Required for NRI Tax Return Filing

A structured checklist to assemble your financial and legal records prior to return preparation and filing.

Identity & Residency Records

  • Indian PAN Card: Essential for filing, claiming TDS credits, and IT portal authentication.
  • Passport Copies: All pages with arrival and departure immigration stamps to calculate physical days in India.
  • Overseas Address Proof: Utility bill, driver's license, or official residency certificate.
  • Overseas Tax ID: Tax Identification Number (TIN/SSN/SIN/Emirates ID) of foreign jurisdiction.

Banking & Tax Certificates

  • Indian Bank Statements: NRO, NRE, and FCNR account statements for the financial year.
  • Form 26AS & AIS/TIS: Downloaded tax credit statement and Annual Information Statement from the portal.
  • Form 16A / TDS Certificates: Issued by banks for interest deductions or tenants for rent.
  • Form 16: From Indian employers if receiving any domestic salary or director's remuneration.

Property & Investment Documentation

  • Rental Lease Agreements: Executed tenancy agreements and proof of municipal property taxes paid.
  • Property Sale & Purchase Deeds: Original purchase deed, registered sale agreement, and Form 16B (buyer TDS).
  • Capital Gains Statements: Annual mutual fund, equity broker statements, or demat ledger sheets.
  • Reinvestment Proof: Section 54EC bond certificates or documentation of new residential property purchase.

Notice: Document requirements vary depending on your specific sources of income, assets held, and whether you are claiming treaty benefits under DTAA. During your preliminary consultation, we supply a customized document checklist tailored to your exact profile.

Frequently Asked Questions — NRI Tax Return Filing

Clear answers to critical legal and tax questions under the Income-tax Act, 1961, and cross-border tax treaties.

An NRI is statutorily required to file an Income Tax Return (ITR) in India under Section 139(1) if their total Indian-source gross taxable income (before deductions) exceeds the basic exemption limit for the relevant financial year (currently ₹2.5 Lakhs under the old tax regime and ₹3 Lakhs under the new tax regime).

Furthermore, even if your total income is below the exemption limit, filing is essential if you wish to claim refunds of excess Tax Deducted at Source (TDS), carry forward short-term or long-term capital losses, or satisfy mandatory financial reporting conditions.

Under Section 5(2) of the Income-tax Act, an NRI is taxed solely on income that is received, accrues, or arises in India. This includes rental income from Indian properties, interest from NRO savings and fixed deposits, capital gains from the sale of Indian property, shares, or mutual funds, and fees for professional services performed in India.

Any income earned abroad from overseas employment, foreign businesses, or international investments is entirely exempt from Indian taxation.

Yes, rental income derived from real estate located in India is fully taxable under the head "Income from House Property". However, NRIs are entitled to a standard statutory deduction of 30% of Net Annual Value under Section 24(a) for repairs and maintenance, regardless of actual expenses incurred.

You can also deduct municipal taxes paid during the year and interest paid on housing loans under Section 24(b). Tenants paying rent to an NRI must deduct TDS at 31.2% under Section 195.

Yes, having an Indian Permanent Account Number (PAN) is mandatory for filing income tax returns, opening NRE/NRO bank accounts, purchasing or selling property, and claiming TDS credits in Form 26AS.

If an NRI does not hold a PAN, banks and property purchasers are statutorily required to deduct TDS at a higher rate (often 20% or more) under Section 206AA. Our chambers can assist NRIs in obtaining a fresh or updated PAN card.

Yes, absolutely. In fact, claiming TDS refunds is one of the primary reasons NRIs file Indian tax returns. Under Section 195, banks automatically deduct 30% plus cess on NRO interest, and property buyers deduct 20% plus surcharges on real estate sales.

If your actual effective tax liability (after basic exemption, slab rates, standard deductions, and capital gains exemptions) is lower than the amount withheld, filing an ITR allows you to claim a full refund directly deposited into your pre-validated Indian bank account.

A Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) is a bilateral tax treaty entered into between India and over 85 foreign nations (including the US, UK, Canada, Australia, Singapore, and UAE) to ensure the same income is not taxed twice.

Under Section 90 of the Income-tax Act, an NRI can claim concessional tax rates or a Foreign Tax Credit (FTC) in their country of residence for taxes paid in India. To avail of DTAA benefits, the non-resident must obtain a Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) from their host country and submit Form 10F online.

Yes. The entire Indian income tax filing procedure is 100% digital. An NRI can complete the consultation, review computation sheets, and authorise electronic filing through our chambers without any physical travel to India.

E-verification of the filed return can be completed remotely using an Electronic Verification Code (EVC) generated through your net banking portal, Aadhaar OTP (if linked to an active Indian mobile), or sending a signed ITR-V copy to CPC Bengaluru.

Primary requirements include your Indian PAN card, passport pages with entry and exit stamps, NRO and NRE bank statements, Form 26AS, and your Annual Information Statement (AIS).

Depending on your income profile, you will also require rent agreements, property municipal tax receipts, property purchase and sale agreements, broker capital gains statements, Form 16A TDS certificates, and your overseas Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) if claiming DTAA relief.

For individual non-residents who are not subject to a statutory tax audit, the standard deadline for filing an annual income tax return is 31st July following the end of the financial year.

If you miss the July deadline, a belated return can still be submitted under Section 139(4) until 31st December of the assessment year upon payment of a late filing fee under Section 234F. Updated returns (ITR-U) can be filed up to 24 months from the end of the assessment year under Section 139(8A).
