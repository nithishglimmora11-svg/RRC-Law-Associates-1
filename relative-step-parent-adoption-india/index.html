Relative and Step-Parent Adoption in India: Procedure and Legal Requirements

A definitive procedural guide examining Chapters V & VI of Adoption Regulations 2022, statutory definition of relatives under Section 2(52) JJ Act, biological parent consent verification, DCPU background reports, and District Magistrate adoption decrees.

Last Updated: 21 November 2026 13 Min Read Statutory Framework: JJ Act 2015 & Reg. 51-55

Essential Intra-Family Adoption Takeaways

Strict Definition of "Relative": Under Section 2(52) of the Juvenile Justice Act, 2015, only paternal uncle or aunt, maternal uncle or aunt, paternal grandparents, or maternal grandparents qualify as "relatives." Cousins, family friends, or distant kin do NOT qualify for the streamlined relative adoption route.

Distinct Step-Parent Regime: Step-parent adoption allows a spouse to legally adopt the biological child of their husband or wife from a previous union. It requires the mandatory consent of both biological parents (including the non-custodial parent, unless deceased or legally dispensed with).

Mandatory CARINGS Registration: Even though the child is a blood relative or step-child, the adoption MUST be registered online on the CARA/CARINGS portal under Chapters V or VI of Adoption Regulations, 2022. Private unregistered handovers are illegal under Section 80.

District Magistrate Decree: The final legal transfer of parentage is granted by the District Magistrate (DM) under Section 61, following a Family Background Report (FBR) submitted by the District Child Protection Unit (DCPU).

1. Introduction to Intra-Family Adoption in India

In Indian society, informal arrangements within extended families—where an uncle, aunt, or grandparent raises a sibling's or child's offspring—have deep cultural roots. However, under modern Indian jurisprudence, an informal family arrangement carries zero legal validity. Without a certified statutory adoption order or registered legal instrument, the child remains the legal dependent of the biological parents, cannot list adoptive parents on passports or school records, and faces severe inheritance disputes upon the demise of senior family members.

To reconcile genuine family solidarity with child protection safeguards, the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015, and the Adoption Regulations, 2022, introduced a dedicated, expedited legal track for two distinct categories: Relative Adoption and Step-Parent Adoption. Understanding the legal divergence between these two routes is crucial for families seeking to formalize parental bonds.

2. What Is Relative Adoption?

Relative adoption occurs when a child whose biological parents are living (or deceased) is formally adopted by an eligible close family member. Under Section 2(52) of the JJ Act, 2015, the term "relative" is legally circumscribed. An applicant qualifies as a "relative" only if they fall within one of the following four degrees:

  • Paternal Uncle or Aunt: The brother or sister of the child's biological father (and their spouse).
  • Maternal Uncle or Aunt: The brother or sister of the child's biological mother (and their spouse).
  • Paternal Grandparents: The parents of the child's biological father.
  • Maternal Grandparents: The parents of the child's biological mother.

Statutory Excerpt: Section 2(52) of the Juvenile Justice Act, 2015

"'Relative', in relation to a child for the purpose of adoption under this Act, means a paternal uncle or aunt, or a maternal uncle or aunt, or paternal grandparent or maternal grandparent."

Crucial Legal Note: First cousins, second cousins, grand-uncles, family friends, or non-relative guardians cannot apply under the relative adoption route. Any adoption involving non-qualifying relatives must proceed through standard institutional adoption channels or HAMA (if both parties are Hindu and meet Section 11 conditions).

3. What Is Step-Parent Adoption?

Step-parent adoption occurs when an individual marries a partner who already has a biological child from a prior marriage or relationship, and seeks to become the child's legal parent alongside their spouse.

Under Chapter VI (Regulations 54 and 55) of the Adoption Regulations, 2022, step-parent adoption produces a unique legal outcome:

  • Preservation of Custodial Parent's Rights: Unlike standard adoptions where all biological ties are severed, step-parent adoption does not sever the parental rights of the biological spouse who has custody.
  • Severance of Non-Custodial Parent's Rights: The adoption permanently terminates the legal rights, custody claims, and maintenance liabilities of the other biological parent (the ex-spouse), transferring full legal parentage to the step-parent.
  • Unified Family Identity: The child acquires the legal surname, succession status, and maintenance rights of the step-parent as if born into the marriage.

4. Applicable Legal Framework: Chapters V & VI of Adoption Regulations, 2022

Unlike institutional adoption where prospective parents must wait on national seniority lists for orphan or abandoned children, relative and step-parent adoptions follow a specialized procedural framework:

5. Strict Biological Parental Consent Requirements

Consent is the absolute cornerstone of intra-family adoption. Any defect, coercion, or ambiguity in parental consent will result in the immediate dismissal of the adoption petition by the District Magistrate.

Relative Adoption Consent:

  • Schedule XX Consent Form: The biological parents must execute the formal consent form prescribed under Schedule XX of the Adoption Regulations, 2022.
  • Joint Biological Consent: Both biological father and biological mother must sign the consent form before a judicial magistrate, notary public, or DCPU officer.
  • Informed Voluntariness: The biological parents must acknowledge in writing that they understand the adoption is permanent and irrevocable, and that they will have no future custody, visitation, or maintenance claims.

Step-Parent Adoption Consent:

  • Consent of Custodial Spouse: The biological parent married to the step-parent must execute explicit written consent approving the adoption.
  • Consent of Non-Custodial Biological Parent: Under Regulation 55, the consent of the other biological parent (the former spouse) is legally mandatory.
  • Dispensing with Non-Custodial Consent: Consent of the non-custodial parent can only be dispensed with if:
    • The non-custodial parent is deceased (evidenced by certified death certificate);
    • The non-custodial parent's rights have been judicially extinguished by a court of competent jurisdiction; or
    • The court or District Magistrate formally dispenses with consent after notice due to complete abandonment or untraceability.

6. Required Documents for Family Adoptions

Applicants must compile an authenticated evidentiary dossier to upload to the CARINGS portal:

Family genealogical tree (Pangali / Vamshavali) proving defined relative relationship
Birth certificate of the child being adopted
Identity proofs (PAN, Aadhaar, Passport) of prospective parents and biological parents
Duly executed Schedule XX Consent Form signed by biological parents
Registered marriage certificate of prospective adoptive parents
Certified copy of divorce decree / death certificate of prior spouse (for step-parent adoptions)
Medical fitness certificates of adoptive parents issued by an MBBS doctor
Written consent of the child if aged five years or older

7. Step-by-Step Procedure on CARINGS

The formal procedure for completing a relative or step-parent adoption encompasses six sequential legal stages:

1

CARINGS Portal Registration

The prospective parents register online on carings.nic.in, selecting the specific module: "Relative Adoption" or "Step-Parent Adoption." They enter the child's details and upload the biological parents' signed Schedule XX consent.

2

Verification by District Child Protection Unit (DCPU)

The file is assigned to the DCPU of the district where the child resides. The Child Protection Officer verifies the genealogical documents and conducts a physical interview with the biological and prospective parents.

3

Preparation of Family Background Report (FBR)

The DCPU prepares a comprehensive Family Background Report (FBR) assessing family harmony, financial adequacy, absence of commercial coercion, and child welfare. The FBR is uploaded to CARINGS within 30 days.

4

Filing Adoption Petition before District Magistrate

The applicants, through legal counsel, file an adoption application under Section 61 of the JJ Act before the jurisdictional District Magistrate, annexing the CARINGS registration certificate and DCPU FBR.

5

In-Camera DM Hearing & Final Adoption Decree

The District Magistrate conducts in-camera proceedings. The DM interacts with the child, verifies biological consent, and issues a final, certified Adoption Order within the statutory 60-day window.

6

Birth Certificate Amendment

The certified adoption decree is submitted to the local Registrar of Births and Deaths. A revised birth certificate is issued recording the adoptive parents as natural parents.

8. Inter-Country Relative Adoption Safeguards

When the prospective adoptive relative is a Non-Resident Indian (NRI), Overseas Citizen of India (OCI), or foreign citizen residing abroad, the adoption becomes cross-border and is subject to Section 60 of the JJ Act and Chapter VII of Adoption Regulations 2022:

  • Home Country Home Study: The overseas relatives must have a Home Study Report prepared by an Authorised Foreign Adoption Agency (AFAA) or Central Authority in their country of residence.
  • Hague Article 15/16 Compliance: If the receiving country is a signatory to the 1993 Hague Convention, the Central Authority of that country must issue formal Article 17 approval.
  • CARA No Objection Certificate (NOC): The District Magistrate in India cannot pass an adoption order for an inter-country relative adoption until CARA issues a statutory No Objection Certificate (NOC) under Regulation 53.

For exhaustive analysis of cross-border adoption workflows, read our companion guide on Inter-Country Adoption in India: NRI & OCI Guide.

9. Common Legal Pitfalls in Family Adoptions

Extended families frequently make costly legal errors when handling intra-family adoptions:

  • Relying on a Notarized Affidavit Alone: Many families sign an informal stamp paper affidavit stating "I give my child to my brother." Such affidavits have zero legal validity under Indian law and leave the child without legal parentage.
  • Adopting Non-Qualifying Relatives: Attempting to adopt a first cousin's child under the relative route. Because cousins fall outside Section 2(52), CARA rejects the application immediately.
  • Proceeding Without Non-Custodial Father's Consent: In step-parent adoptions, proceeding without formal service of notice or consent of the biological father leads to immediate writ petitions (habeas corpus) in the High Court.

Legal Warning: Custody Litigation Risks

"Informal family custody transfers can be revoked at any time by the biological parents. Only a certified Adoption Order passed by the District Magistrate legally extinguishes biological parentage and secures permanent parental rights."

11. Conclusion

Relative and step-parent adoptions strengthen family continuity and offer vulnerable children a safe, permanent upbringing within their biological kin network. However, because informal custody transfers carry no legal weight and leave families vulnerable to future custody litigation, completing the formal statutory process under Chapters V and VI of Adoption Regulations 2022 is vital. Partnering with experienced family law counsel ensures that parental consents are legally unchallengeable and the District Magistrate decree is secured expeditiously.
