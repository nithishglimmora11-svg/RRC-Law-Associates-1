Inter-Country Adoption in India: Legal Procedure for NRIs, OCIs and Foreign Residents

An authoritative legal treatise examining inter-country adoption under Sections 56(4), 59 & 60 of the Juvenile Justice Act, 2015, the 1993 Hague Convention safeguards, Authorised Foreign Adoption Agencies (AFAA), CARA No Objection Certificates, and overseas immigration visas.

Last Updated: 21 November 2026 16 Min Read Statutory Framework: JJ Act 2015 & Hague 1993

Essential Inter-Country Adoption Takeaways

Statutory Priority Order (Section 59(1)): Under the Juvenile Justice Act, children are placed in a strict hierarchy of priority: (1) Resident Indian citizens; (2) Non-Resident Indians (NRIs); (3) Overseas Citizens of India (OCIs); and (4) Foreign prospective adoptive parents.

Mandatory Hague Convention Compliance: Inter-country adoptions from India are governed by the 1993 Hague Adoption Convention. The process requires bilateral approval: Home Country Central Authority (Article 17 Agreement) and India's CARA (No Objection Certificate).

Role of Authorised Foreign Adoption Agencies (AFAA): Prospective parents living abroad cannot apply directly to an Indian orphanage; they must register through an accredited AFAA or government Central Authority in their country of habitual residence.

Statutory Pathway Under Chapter VIII for HAMA: Eligible Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) and Overseas Citizens of India (OCIs) who adopt a Hindu child under HAMA in India can legally seek international relocation by complying with Chapter VIII (Regulations 64–70) of Adoption Regulations, 2022, which requires Designated Portal registration, foreign home study report, District Magistrate verification, and CARA certification.

1. Introduction to Inter-Country Adoption

Inter-country adoption involves the lawful transfer of a child from their country of origin (India) to a receiving country where the prospective adoptive parents habitually reside. Because cross-border adoptions traverse multiple legal jurisdictions, international boundaries, and immigration regimes, they are subject to the highest level of statutory scrutiny to prevent child trafficking, unauthorized profit, and child abduction.

In India, the overarching principle governing inter-country adoption is the principle of subsidiarity, recognized in Article 21 of the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child (UNCRC) and the 1993 Hague Convention on Protection of Children and Co-operation in Respect of Intercountry Adoption. Under this doctrine, inter-country adoption is recognized as a valid family option only after all reasonable efforts to place the child with a suitable family in India have been exhausted, except in cases of relative adoptions.

2. Legal Framework in India: Sections 56(4), 59 & 60 of the JJ Act

Inter-country adoptions are regulated under Chapter VIII of the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015, and Chapter VII of the Adoption Regulations, 2022:

  • Section 56(4) JJ Act: Expressly mandates that all inter-country adoptions of orphan, abandoned, or surrendered children, as well as children of relatives, must be completed in accordance with the provisions of the Act and the regulations framed by CARA.
  • Section 59 JJ Act: Governs the institutional procedure for inter-country adoption of orphan, abandoned, and surrendered children. It establishes the priority hierarchy, foreign home study requirements, and issuance of No Objection Certificates (NOC).
  • Section 60 JJ Act: Governs inter-country relative adoptions, establishing a streamlined procedure where an Indian child is adopted by close relatives residing abroad.

Statutory Excerpt: Section 56(4) of the Juvenile Justice Act, 2015

"All inter-country adoptions shall be done only as per the provisions of this Act and the adoption regulations framed by the Authority."

3. The 1993 Hague Convention on Intercountry Adoption

India ratified the Hague Convention on Protection of Children and Co-operation in Respect of Intercountry Adoption in 2003. The Convention creates a binding multilateral treaty framework between India and more than 100 contracting states (including the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, Germany, and New Zealand):

  • Article 15 (Home Study Report): The receiving country's Central Authority must certify that the prospective adoptive parents are eligible and suited to adopt under the laws of their home country.
  • Article 16 (Child Dossier): CARA must verify that the child is legally free for adoption, that biological consents were freely obtained without commercial inducement, and that inter-country placement serves the child's best interests.
  • Article 17 (Mutual Agreement to Proceed): The adoption cannot proceed until the Central Authorities of both countries have formally agreed that the placement may proceed.
  • Article 23 (Conformity Certificate): Upon the grant of the final adoption order by the District Magistrate in India, CARA issues a certified Hague Conformity Certificate, which guarantees automatic legal recognition in all other Hague signatory states.

4. Distinct Categories of Overseas Applicants

The legal rights, processing timelines, and statutory documentation requirements vary considerably depending on the prospective parents' citizenship and immigration status:

Applicant Category Citizenship Status Statutory Priority under Sec. 59(1) Immigration Verification
Non-Resident Indian (NRI) Indian Passport Holder residing abroad First priority among overseas applicants (treated on par with resident Indians after local matching) Valid foreign residency permit / green card / work visa
Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) Foreign Passport Holder with valid OCI Card Second priority among overseas applicants Foreign passport and registered OCI Card
Foreign Nationals Foreign Passport Holder with no Indian origin Third priority (eligible primarily for older children, sibling groups, or special needs) Foreign citizenship certificate and full home country clearance

5. Statutory Priority Order under Section 59(1)

Under Section 59(1) of the JJ Act, children declared legally free for adoption in India are offered for placement according to a strict legal hierarchy:

  • Priority 1: Resident Indian Citizens: If a child can be rehabilitated within an Indian family living in India, institutional policy mandates domestic placement.
  • Priority 2: Non-Resident Indians (NRIs): If no suitable resident Indian family accepts the child within 60 days of the child being placed on the CARINGS national database, the child is made available to NRIs.
  • Priority 3: Overseas Citizens of India (OCIs): If no NRI family accepts the child, the referral is extended to OCI cardholders.
  • Priority 4: Foreign Nationals: Foreign applicants generally receive referrals for children who have not found domestic placement, sibling groups, or children with certified special medical needs.

6. Authorised Foreign Adoption Agencies (AFAA) & Central Authorities

Under Regulation 34 of Adoption Regulations 2022, prospective parents living abroad cannot engage directly with Indian child care institutions or individual lawyers to source a child. All applications must originate through an authorized institutional intermediary:

  • Authorised Foreign Adoption Agency (AFAA): A licensed child-placement agency accredited by the government of the receiving country and recognized by CARA in India.
  • Central Authority (CA): In countries without accredited private agencies, the government ministry or department responsible for child welfare (e.g., Department of State in the US, Department for Education in the UK) acts as the coordinating authority.
  • Responsibilities: The AFAA or Central Authority conducts the comprehensive Home Study Report in the prospective parents' home country, verifies police clearances, coordinates the Article 17 approval, and monitors post-adoption follow-up reports.

7. Step-by-Step Procedure for NRIs and OCIs Living Abroad

The inter-country adoption workflow comprises twelve structured legal phases:

1

Engagement with AFAA or Central Authority

Prospective parents contact an accredited Authorised Foreign Adoption Agency (AFAA) or government Central Authority in their country of habitual residence to commence the home study.

2

Overseas Home Study Report (HSR) Preparation

A licensed foreign social worker assesses the applicants' home environment, psychological health, financial solvency, and criminal background checks (FBI/RCMP/DBS), preparing the HSR under Article 15.

3

CARINGS Online Registration

The AFAA uploads the verified Home Study Report, dossiers, and KYC documents to the CARINGS portal (carings.nic.in) under the "Inter-Country Adoption" module.

4

Seniority Generation & Referral Matching

CARA scrutinizes the file and establishes the overseas applicants' seniority date. When a matching child becomes available under the priority schedule, a referral is generated.

5

96-Hour Referral Review Window

Under Chapter IV of Adoption Regulations 2022, overseas prospective adoptive parents review the Child Study Report (CSR) and Medical Examination Report (MER) in consultation with their Authorised Foreign Adoption Agency or Central Authority, distinguished from the 48-hour domestic reservation window under Regulation 11(3).

6

Article 17 Hague Agreement to Proceed

Upon the parents' acceptance, the Central Authority of the receiving country issues a formal Article 17 Agreement confirming that the child will be permitted to enter and reside permanently in the receiving state.

7

Issuance of CARA No Objection Certificate (NOC)

Under Regulation 38, CARA's statutory committee reviews the Article 17 approval and CSR, issuing a formal No Objection Certificate (NOC) permitting the adoption to proceed in India.

8

Filing Adoption Application before District Magistrate

The Specialised Adoption Agency (SAA) in India files the formal adoption petition under Section 59(7) of the JJ Act before the jurisdictional District Magistrate.

9

In-Camera DM Hearing & Final Adoption Decree

The District Magistrate conducts in-camera proceedings (PAPs may appear in person or via approved video conference) and passes the final Adoption Order within 60 days.

10

Issuance of Hague Conformity Certificate (Article 23)

The SAA submits the certified DM order to CARA. CARA issues the certified Hague Conformity Certificate confirming that the adoption was executed in strict adherence to treaty standards.

11

Passport & Adoption Entry Visa Issuance

The SAA applies for a Tatkaal Indian Passport for the child with the Regional Passport Office (RPO). The adoptive parents then submit the adoption order and Conformity Certificate to their embassy for an adoption visa (e.g., IH-3 / IH-4 for USA).

12

Travel Home & Post-Adoption Quarterly Reporting

The adoptive parents travel with the child to their home country. The AFAA submits progress reports to CARINGS quarterly during the first year and bi-annually during the second year.

8. Inter-Country Relative Adoption Framework (Section 60)

Section 60 of the JJ Act provides a specialized legal channel for Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) and Overseas Citizens of India (OCIs) seeking to adopt a biological relative's child from India:

  • Relationship Definition: The child must fall strictly within the defined kinship of Section 2(52) (brother/sister's child or grandchild).
  • Exemption from General Queue: The overseas relative does not wait in the national queue for unrelated infants.
  • Mandatory CARA NOC: Under Regulation 53, even in relative cases, the District Magistrate cannot pass an adoption order until CARA issues a formal No Objection Certificate after verifying Hague Article 15/17 compliance.

9. Inter-Country Adoption Under HAMA: Statutory Procedure Under Chapter VIII of Adoption Regulations, 2022

A critical distinction in Indian adoption law exists between domestic Hindu adoptions and cross-border relocations. While Section 56(3) of the JJ Act preserves the operation of the Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956 (HAMA) for domestic adoptions between Hindus residing in India, Section 56(4) establishes that all inter-country adoptions must comply with the regulations framed by CARA.

To resolve cross-border relocation issues for the Indian diaspora, the Ministry of Women and Child Development notified Chapter VIII (Regulations 64 to 70) of the Adoption Regulations, 2022, titled "Adoption of Child Under Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956 by Prospective Adoptive Parents Habitually Residing Abroad".

This Chapter provides an official, statutory pathway for eligible Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) and Overseas Citizens of India (OCIs) who have adopted a child under HAMA:

  • Registration on the Designated Portal (Regulation 65): The prospective adoptive parents must register on CARINGS, uploading the registered HAMA adoption deed, child birth certificate, biological parents' consent, and relevant identity documents.
  • Foreign Home Study Report & Receiving Country Clearance (Regulation 66): A Home Study Report must be prepared by an Authorised Foreign Adoption Agency (AFAA) or Central Authority of the receiving country, confirming the suitability of the prospective parents and containing an endorsement, letter of support, or permission from the receiving country's government authority for the child's entry and permanent residence under applicable domestic and Hague Convention laws.
  • District Magistrate Inquiry and Verification (Regulation 67): The District Magistrate of the district where the HAMA adoption was registered conducts a thorough inquiry to verify that the requirements of HAMA (Sections 6 to 11) were strictly satisfied, that natural parents gave voluntary consent without consideration under Section 17, and that the adoption is genuine. The DM submits an official verification report to CARA.
  • CARA Approval & Certificate of Adoption/Conformity (Regulations 68 & 69): Upon reviewing the District Magistrate's verification report and foreign authority clearance, CARA issues a Certificate of Adoption / Conformity under Chapter VIII, enabling passport issuance and international visa processing.

Statutory Framework: Chapter VIII of Adoption Regulations, 2022

Chapter VIII (Regulations 64–70) establishes the lawful regulatory procedure for NRIs and OCIs relocating a child adopted under HAMA. Attempting to take a child abroad relying solely on a registered HAMA deed without completing the Chapter VIII Designated Portal verification will result in the refusal of adoption entry visas by foreign immigration authorities (such as US USCIS or UK Home Office).

11. Conclusion

Inter-country adoption unites global Indian families while providing loving homes to deserving children. However, because cross-border adoptions sit at the complex intersection of Indian statutory law, international treaty obligations, and receiving-country immigration mandates, flawless procedural execution is essential. Engaging knowledgeable legal counsel with cross-border expertise guarantees that your adoption complies with CARA regulations, satisfies Hague Convention benchmarks, and secures unconditional immigration clearance for your child.
