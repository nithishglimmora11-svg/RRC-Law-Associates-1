Customs Show Cause Notice: How to Prepare an Effective Legal Reply

Strategic Defense, Statutory Limitation, Evidence Gathering and Adjudication Procedure Under the Customs Act, 1962

11 min read
Reviewed by RRC Customs Litigation Practice
Updated November 2026

Key Takeaways & At a Glance

  • A Customs Show Cause Notice (SCN) is a mandatory statutory instrument under Section 28 or Section 124; failure to reply comprehensively within the stipulated period can lead to ex-parte orders of confiscation and heavy penalties.
  • Always inspect and verify the Relied Upon Documents (RUDs). If documents cited in the SCN annexure were not furnished, immediately file a formal request for inspection and copies before submitting the reply on merits.
  • Verify whether the demand is barred by statutory limitation. Section 28(1) prescribes a 2-year limitation for normal demands, whereas Section 28(4) requires the department to prove deliberate collusion, fraud, or suppression of facts to invoke the 5-year extended window.
  • Never tender a generic denial. Structure your legal reply with preliminary statutory objections, jurisdictional challenges, paragraph-by-paragraph factual rebuttals, technical classification evidence, and an explicit prayer for personal hearing.
  • Adjudicating authorities are mandated under Section 122A to grant a personal hearing (with a maximum of three adjournments upon sufficient cause) before passing a reasoned, speaking order.

Understanding the Legal Issue

When the Indian Customs Department suspects an under-valuation, incorrect classification, irregular duty exemption, or non-compliance during import or export, it initiates formal proceedings by issuing a Show Cause Notice (SCN). An SCN is not an arbitrary communication or a casual query; it is a foundational legal document that crystallizes the department's allegations, quantifies the proposed duty demand, and outlines proposed penalties and confiscation.

For importers, manufacturers, customs brokers, and international traders across Tamil Nadu and Pan-India, receiving an SCN marks the beginning of formal adversarial quasi-judicial proceedings. Under the principles of natural justice, the recipient is afforded a reasonable opportunity to demonstrate why the proposed actions—such as duty recovery under Section 28, confiscation of consignments under Section 111 or 113, and severe personal penalties under Section 112 or 114A—should not be confirmed. Preparing an effective, airtight reply requires both rigorous factual verification and sound statutory interpretation.

Key Provisions of the Customs Act, 1962

Customs proceedings in India are governed strictly by codified parliamentary statutes and binding notifications issued by the Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC). The foundational statutory provisions applicable to this matter include:

Section 124 Customs Act, 1962

Mandates the issuance of a written notice specifying grounds, granting reasonable time to reply, and providing an opportunity of being heard before ordering confiscation or penalty.

Section 28 Customs Act, 1962

Statutory mechanism for recovery of duties not levied, not paid, or short-levied; establishes the 2-year normal limitation and 5-year extended period for fraud or suppression.

Section 122A Customs Act, 1962

Codifies adjudication procedure, mandating adherence to natural justice and permitting an adjudicating authority to grant a maximum of three adjournments upon reasonable cause.

In-Depth Legal Analysis & Practical Application

1. Analyzing the Allegations & Relied Upon Documents (RUDs)

Every Show Cause Notice contains a narrative statement of facts accompanied by an annexure listing 'Relied Upon Documents' (RUDs). The supreme principle of natural justice requires that the adjudicating department provide clear, legible copies of all RUDs to the noticee. If investigative statements recorded under Section 108, laboratory test reports, overseas supplier inquiries, or DRI intelligence reports are referenced but not supplied, the noticee has an absolute legal right to demand complete inspection before filing a reply on merits.

2. Formulating Preliminary Legal & Jurisdictional Objections

Before addressing the substantive factual merits of the case, an effective legal reply raises preliminary jurisdictional and procedural hurdles:

  • Competence of the Proper Officer: Verifying whether the issuing officer possesses territorial and monetary jurisdiction under CBIC notifications (especially relevant following judicial rulings on DRI and preventive officer jurisdiction).
  • Bar of Statutory Limitation: Demonstrating that the facts were already within the department's knowledge (e.g., through self-assessment, bills of entry, or audit queries), thereby invalidating the invocation of Section 28(4) extended limitation.
  • Vagueness of Allegations: Highlighting instances where allegations lack specific evidence, calculations, or clear legal foundation.

3. Substantive Rebuttal: Classification, Valuation, and Evidence

The factual merits must address each numbered paragraph in the SCN methodically:

  • HSN Classification: Relying on General Rules for the Interpretation (GRI) of the Customs Tariff, Section and Chapter Notes, World Customs Organization (WCO) Explanatory Notes, and CAAR Advance Rulings.
  • Valuation Disputes: Demonstrating compliance with Rule 3 of the Customs Valuation Rules, 2007, and defending the declared transaction value against arbitrary upliftment.
  • Rebutting Mens Rea / Intent: Establishing that the importer acted in good faith with full disclosure, thereby dismantling the foundation for penalties under Section 112 or Section 114A.

Step-by-Step Legal Procedure

Navigating customs defense requires strict adherence to statutory sequences. The standard operational procedure followed by experienced customs litigation counsel includes:

Statutory SCN Defense Workflow

Procedural Sequence: Customs Show Cause Notice to Final Adjudication

1

Service & Limitation Audit

Verify date of service and ascertain whether demand falls within Section 28(1) normal limitation (2 years) or Section 28(4) extended limitation (5 years).

2

RUD Inspection & Requisition

Compare SCN annexure with received documents; file formal interim request for unsupplied Relied Upon Documents and non-relied exculpatory records.

3

Factual & Statutory Reply

Submit comprehensive paragraph-by-paragraph reply raising jurisdictional objections, classification evidence, and demanding an oral hearing under Section 122A.

4

Personal Hearing & Order

Argue legal submissions before the Adjudicating Commissioner; submit written submissions before the passing of a reasoned, speaking order.

1

Check Date of Service & Statutory Deadlines

Record the exact date and mode of service (postal, email, or speed post) to compute the response deadline (typically 30 days).

2

Document Audit & RUD Verification

Compare the SCN annexure against received documents. Immediately submit an interim letter requesting any missing RUDs or non-relied documents required for defense.

3

Technical & Commercial Fact-Gathering

Assemble bills of entry, commercial invoices, packing lists, test certificates, foreign remittances, and statutory accounting records.

4

Drafting Legal Reply with Structured Annexures

Draft preliminary statutory objections followed by paragraph-by-paragraph replies, statutory case law precedents, and certified annexures.

5

Filing Formal Reply & Requesting Personal Hearing

File the reply with official acknowledgment stamp; explicitly reserve the right to tender oral arguments and file additional written submissions at the Section 122A hearing.

6

Advocacy at Personal Hearing

Present legal and technical arguments before the adjudicating authority, ensuring all verbal submissions are recorded in the official Record of Personal Hearing.

Essential Documents & Evidence Checklist

To substantiate claims, establish commercial bonafides, and refute departmental allegations, assemble the following documentary evidence:

  • Original Show Cause Notice along with all annexures and demand quantification sheets
  • Copy of relevant Bills of Entry, Shipping Bills, and electronic ICEGATE acknowledgments
  • Commercial invoices, packing lists, airway bills, or bills of lading
  • Technical product specifications, laboratory test certificates, and manufacturer catalogs
  • Bank realization certificates (BRCs) or foreign remittance proofs (AD Code / Swift copies)
  • Previous assessment orders, SVB orders, or related appellate tribunal decisions

Practical Case Scenario

Scenario: A Chennai-based manufacturing company imported telecommunication networking equipment under an exemption notification. Three years after clearance, the Directorate of Revenue Intelligence (DRI) issued an SCN demanding ₹1.8 Crores in differential duty under Section 28(4), alleging suppression of technical features and proposing 100% penalties under Section 114A.

Legal Resolution: RRC Law Associates reviewed the self-assessment records and discovered that the exact technical catalog and descriptive invoices had been uploaded on ICEGATE during initial clearance. In the legal reply, our advocates established that all technical parameters were fully disclosed upon filing, meaning there was zero suppression of facts. As a result, the invocation of the 5-year extended limitation under Section 28(4) was legally unsustainable. At the personal hearing under Section 122A, the adjudicating authority accepted the limitation defense, dropping the time-barred demand and setting aside all proposed penalties.

Common Legal Pitfalls to Avoid

Critical Procedural Mistakes

  • Ignoring the SCN or requesting repeated informal extensions without filing a formal interim letter on record.
  • Filing a generic denial without specifically traversing each numbered allegation and demand calculation.
  • Admitting facts or calculations in the written reply that were never proven by the department.
  • Failing to request complete copies of Relied Upon Documents (RUDs) prior to filing the substantive defense.
  • Omitting an explicit written prayer for a Personal Hearing under Section 122A.

Conclusion & Strategic Advice

Customs law disputes in India require swift, technically informed, and legally verified advocacy. Because customs authorities operate under stringent statutory limitation deadlines—from the 30-day Show Cause Notice window to the strict 60-day appellate period under Section 128—delay or informal verbal negotiations frequently compromise substantive legal rights.

Engaging specialized customs legal counsel at the earliest stage of search, summons, seizure, or notice issuance ensures that your business preserves documentary evidence, maintains procedural compliance, and enforces all statutory protections guaranteed under Indian law.
