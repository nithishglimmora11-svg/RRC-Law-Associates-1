CARA Adoption Process in India: Registration, Home Study and Adoption Order

A complete procedural roadmap detailing online CARINGS registration, Specialised Adoption Agency (SAA) home visits, child referral protocols, pre-adoption foster care, and District Magistrate in-camera hearings.

Last Updated: 21 November 2026 14 Min Read Nodal Framework: CARA / CARINGS Portal

Essential CARA Workflow Takeaways

Centralized Portal Governance: All institutional, relative, and inter-country adoptions under the Juvenile Justice framework must be registered through the Child Adoption Resource Information and Guidance System (CARINGS) at carings.nic.in.

Strict Home Study Timeline: Upon registration, an authorized Specialised Adoption Agency (SAA) or District Child Protection Unit (DCPU) must conduct a Home Study and upload the report within sixty days from the date of submission of requisite documents on the Designated Portal, as mandated under Regulation 10(8). Under Regulation 10(10), the Home Study Report remains valid for 3 years.

In-Country 48-Hour Reservation (Regulation 11(3)): For in-country adoption, when a child is algorithmically matched based on seniority and preferences, Prospective Adoptive Parents (PAPs) have forty-eight hours to inspect medical and child study reports and reserve the child through the Designated Portal. Subsequent matching and acceptance are governed separately under Regulation 12.

District Magistrate In-Camera Hearing: Following the 2021 JJ Amendment Act, adoption proceedings under the Juvenile Justice framework are adjudicated by the District Magistrate (DM) under Section 61 of the JJ Act, with a statutory mandate for disposal within two months from filing.

1. What Is CARA?

The Central Adoption Resource Authority (CARA) is an autonomous statutory body established under Section 68 of the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015, functioning under the Ministry of Women and Child Development (MWCD), Government of India.

CARA was created to eliminate clandestine, commercial, and irregular adoptions by functioning as the nodal authority for all in-country adoptions and as the designated Central Authority for inter-country adoptions under the 1993 Hague Convention on Protection of Children and Co-operation in Respect of Intercountry Adoption. Under Section 68(c) of the JJ Act, CARA is empowered to frame regulations, issue operational guidelines, monitor Specialised Adoption Agencies (SAAs), and maintain an unalterable digital audit trail of every child declared legally free for adoption.

2. What Is the CARINGS Portal?

The Child Adoption Resource Information and Guidance System (CARINGS) is the designated digital portal (carings.nic.in) through which all adoption procedures in India are officially administered. Prior to the computerization of adoption, private institutions maintained local waiting lists, leading to widespread allegations of favoritism, irregular placements, and financial exploitation.

CARINGS provides a secure, encrypted online architecture that accomplishes three statutory functions:

  • Child Rehabilitation Registry: Child Care Institutions (CCIs) and SAAs upload profiles of orphan, abandoned, and surrendered children only after the Child Welfare Committee (CWC) issues a formal order declaring them legally free for adoption under Section 38 of the JJ Act.
  • Prospective Parent Central Registry: Maintains a transparent, national seniority list of registered applicants based strictly on their date of document submission and Home Study Report approval.
  • Automated Matching Algorithm: Matches children with waiting parents based on statutory composite age criteria, medical categories, and geographic preferences without human discretion or manual intervention.

3. Who Needs to Register on CARINGS?

Under the Adoption Regulations, 2022, mandatory portal registration applies to four categories of prospective parents:

  • Resident Indian Citizens: Couples or single parents living in India seeking to adopt an unrelated orphan, abandoned, or surrendered infant or older child.
  • Relative Adopters: Resident Indians or Non-Resident Indians adopting a child of their biological relatives as defined under Section 2(52) of the JJ Act.
  • Step-Parents: A husband or wife seeking to adopt the biological child of their legal spouse from a previous marriage under Chapter VI of Adoption Regulations 2022.
  • NRIs, OCIs, and Foreign Nationals: Overseas applicants adopting under the inter-country adoption framework governed by Chapter VII and the Hague Convention.

Statutory Caveat: Direct adoptions executed between natural Hindu parents under the personal law of the Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956 (HAMA) do not require CARINGS registration, as explicitly preserved by Section 56(3) of the JJ Act.

4. The Complete Step-by-Step CARA Adoption Workflow

The institutional adoption journey under the Adoption Regulations, 2022, follows ten sequential statutory milestones:

1

Online Registration & Account Creation

Prospective parents visit carings.nic.in, select their applicant category (In-country, Relative, Step-parent, or Inter-country), fill out basic biographical information, and receive a permanent registration number.

2

Uploading Verified Identification & KYC Documents

Within 30 days of registration, applicants must upload scanned copies of their identity proofs, marriage certificates, medical fitness certificates, and financial proofs to validate their file.

3

Selecting SAA & Home Study Report (HSR)

Applicants select a licensed Specialised Adoption Agency (SAA) in their home district. A certified social worker conducts a home inspection and interviews family members, submitting the completed Home Study Report within sixty days from the date of submission of requisite documents on the Designated Portal, as prescribed under Regulation 10(8). Under Regulation 10(10), the HSR remains valid for three years.

4

Fixing Seniority on CARINGS

Once the SAA approves the HSR on the portal, the applicant's seniority date is officially confirmed. The applicant is placed in the active national waiting queue for their preferred child age category.

5

Algorithmic Child Referral

When a matching child becomes legally free, the CARINGS algorithm generates an automated referral, displaying the Child Study Report (CSR) and Medical Examination Report (MER) to the applicants.

6

In-Country 48-Hour Reservation Window (Regulation 11(3))

Under Regulation 11(3), in-country prospective adoptive parents have forty-eight hours from the time of referral to review the medical and child study reports and reserve the child through the Designated Portal. Referral timelines for inter-country adoption are governed separately under Chapter IV.

7

Physical Agency Visit & Child Acceptance

PAPs travel to the specialized agency where the child resides. PAPs visit the Specialised Adoption Agency to meet the child. Under Regulation 12, the agency facilitates compatibility and matching, whereupon the parents sign the formal Child Acceptance Form (Schedule VII).

8

Pre-Adoption Foster Care Agreement

Under Regulation 13, the SAA and prospective parents execute a Pre-Adoption Foster Care Agreement (Schedule VIII). The parents take temporary physical custody of the child while the legal petition is filed.

9

District Magistrate Hearing & Certified Order

The SAA files an adoption application under Section 61 before the District Magistrate. The DM conducts an in-camera hearing and issues a final, certified Adoption Order within 60 days.

10

New Birth Certificate & Post-Adoption Follow-Up

The local birth registry issues a new birth certificate showing the adoptive parents as legal parents. The SAA conducts mandatory post-adoption follow-ups every 6 months for 2 years.

5. Required Documents for CARINGS Registration

To ensure that an application is not placed on hold or rejected by the agency social worker, PAPs must upload the following authenticated documents:

Recent postcard-sized family photograph of prospective adoptive parents
PAN Card and Aadhaar Card / Passport for identity and citizenship proof
Birth certificates or 10th standard matriculation certificates proving age
Marriage certificate (if married) or divorce decree/death certificate
Medical fitness certificate issued by an MBBS doctor in Schedule VI format
Salary slips, Form 16, or Income Tax Returns for the last 3 financial years
Proof of residence (registered sale deed, electricity bill, or rent deed)
Two recommendation letters from respectable community members

6. The Home Study Report (HSR) Protocol

Under Regulation 10(8) of the Adoption Regulations, 2022, the Home Study Report (HSR) of prospective adoptive parents must be completed within sixty days from the date of submission of requisite documents on the Designated Portal. Under Regulation 10(10), the Home Study Report remains valid for a period of three years and forms the basis for the adoption of a child. The HSR is an exhaustive socio-psychological evaluation:

  • Home Inspection: A qualified social worker from the designated SAA or DCPU visits the applicant's home to inspect living space, sanitation, safety, and physical neighborhood suitability.
  • Family Dynamics Interview: The social worker interviews the applicants, existing children, and extended family members living in the home to assess their psychological readiness and motivation for adoption.
  • 60-Day Mandatory Completion: Under Regulation 10(8), the Specialised Adoption Agency or District Child Protection Unit is statutorily mandated to complete and upload the HSR to the Designated Portal within sixty days from the date of submission of requisite documents.
  • Three-Year Validity: Once approved, the HSR remains legally valid for three years. If an adoption is not finalized within three years, an updated HSR must be conducted.

Statutory Excerpt: Regulation 10(8) & 10(10) of Adoption Regulations, 2022

"Regulation 10(8): The Home study report of the prospective adoptive parents shall be completed within sixty days from the date of submission of requisite documents on the Designated Portal."
"Regulation 10(10): The Home study report shall remain valid for a period of three years and the basis for adoption of a child shall be the home study report."

7. Child Referral, Reservation, and Matching Procedures

Once an applicant's seniority is established on the Designated Portal, the referral, reservation, and matching process operates through distinct statutory stages:

  • Algorithmic Referral: CARINGS generates automated referral alerts when a child declared legally free by the Child Welfare Committee matches the prospective parents' criteria and seniority.
  • Access to CSR and MER: Prospective parents receive secure access to the Child Study Report (CSR) and Medical Examination Report (MER) detailing the child's developmental and medical profile.
  • In-Country 48-Hour Reservation (Regulation 11(3)): Under Regulation 11(3), in-country prospective adoptive parents must reserve the referred child through the Designated Portal within forty-eight hours from the time of referral. If they fail to do so, the child is automatically released to the next applicant in the seniority list. Under Regulation 11(4), if parents allow two referral opportunities to lapse without reservation, they are placed at the bottom of the seniority list.
  • Subsequent Matching and Acceptance (Regulation 12): Following online reservation, the matching procedure is carried out separately under Regulation 12. The Specialised Adoption Agency arranges physical counseling and interaction between the child and prospective parents, and upon mutual compatibility, the Child Acceptance Form (Schedule VII) is executed.
  • Pre-Adoption Foster Care (Regulation 13): Following formal acceptance under Regulation 12, the prospective parents and agency execute a Pre-Adoption Foster Care Agreement under Regulation 13 (Schedule VIII), allowing temporary physical custody pending final legal orders.
  • Distinction for Inter-Country Adoption: The forty-eight hour reservation deadline under Regulation 11(3) applies specifically to in-country adoptions. Referral timelines for inter-country adoption are governed separately under Chapter IV (Regulation 37), allowing overseas applicants tailored review periods through their Authorised Foreign Adoption Agency or Central Authority.

8. Pre-Adoption Foster Care Agreement

Under Regulation 13, once prospective adoptive parents formally accept the child at the agency, the SAA enters into a Pre-Adoption Foster Care Agreement (Schedule VIII). This legal agreement permits the adoptive parents to take the child home immediately, even before the District Magistrate has passed the final legal order.

During this foster period, the parents are legal custodians with full responsibility for medical care, nutrition, and safety, while legal parentage remains under the jurisdiction of the Child Welfare Committee until the DM issues the final decree.

9. District Magistrate Adoption Proceedings

Under Section 61 of the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015, as amended by the Juvenile Justice Amendment Act, 2021, jurisdiction to adjudicate adoption applications under the Juvenile Justice framework was transferred from Civil District Courts to the District Magistrate (DM) and Additional District Magistrates (ADM). This statutory framework applies specifically to adoption proceedings under the JJ Act (concerning orphan, abandoned, surrendered, relative, and step-children processed under the Act). It does not govern domestic Hindu adoptions under HAMA, which are executed through registered deeds without requiring an adoption order under Section 61. For proceedings under the JJ Act, Section 61 prescribes the following procedural safeguards:

  • Filing by SAA: The Specialized Adoption Agency files the adoption application under Section 61 on CARINGS, which is routed electronically to the jurisdictional District Magistrate.
  • 60-Day Disposal Rule: Under Section 61(2) of the JJ Act, adoption proceedings shall be held in camera and the application shall be disposed of by the District Magistrate within a period of two months from the date of filing.
  • In-Camera Examination: The hearing is held in confidential chambers. The DM examines the biological parent surrender deeds, CWC legally-free declarations, HSR, and verified consents.
  • Finality of Order: The certified adoption order issued by the District Magistrate constitutes final, conclusive proof of legal parentage under Indian law.

Statutory Disposal Mandate: Section 61(2) JJ Act, 2015

"The adoption proceedings shall be held in camera and the case shall be disposed of by the District Magistrate within a period of two months from the date of filing of the application."

10. Post-Adoption Follow-Up and Reporting

Legal oversight does not terminate with the District Magistrate's order. To verify that the adopted child is flourishing in the new family environment, Section 68 and Regulation 18 enforce a mandatory post-adoption follow-up protocol:

  • Bi-Annual Agency Reports: A certified social worker from the SAA visits the family residence every six months for a period of two years following the adoption order.
  • Reporting Metrics: The social worker prepares a standardized progress report evaluating the child's physical growth, emotional bonding, schooling, healthcare vaccinations, and overall integration into the household.
  • Portal Upload: All four progress reports are uploaded directly to the CARINGS database, completing the institutional supervision phase.

11. Common Delays, Mistakes & Avoiding Middlemen

Prospective parents frequently encounter avoidable setbacks due to procedural missteps:

  • Allowing Documents to Expire: Medical certificates and financial proofs have statutory expiration windows. Failing to upload renewed records causes automatic portal freezes.
  • Falling Prey to Unofficial Touts: Unscrupulous touts and private hospital fixers often promise "instant private adoptions." Accepting a child outside CARINGS exposes parents to criminal prosecution under Section 80 of the JJ Act.
  • Ignoring the 48-Hour Clock: Failing to monitor registered emails and phone numbers during the referral window can result in an accidental lapse of a long-awaited child match.

13. Conclusion

The CARA adoption process represents India's commitment to ensuring that every adoption is child-centric, secure, and legally transparent. By strictly following CARINGS protocols—from HSR completion through to the District Magistrate's order—prospective parents establish an unbreakable legal bond with their child. Partnering with a dedicated legal team ensures that your documentation complies with every statutory regulation, safeguarding your family against delays and administrative pitfalls.
