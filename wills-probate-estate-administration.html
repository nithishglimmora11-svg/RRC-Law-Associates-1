Preventative Succession Planning

Custom Will Drafting & Testamentary Protection

A carefully structured Will is the primary instrument to safeguard your generational wealth, real estate holdings, and financial investments, ensuring your assets pass to your intended beneficiaries without protracted civil litigation.

Under the Indian Succession Act, 1925, a valid Will requires a testator of sound disposing mind executing the instrument in the presence of at least two independent attesting witnesses. At RRC Law Associates, we guide clients through comprehensive testamentary structuring:

  • Custom Will Drafting & Codicils: Explicit allocation of self-acquired movable and immovable properties, testamentary guardianship for minor children, creation of life estates, and residual beneficiary clauses.
  • Preventing Common Will Contests: Drafting proactive provisions that address potential claims of undue influence, coercion, or lack of capacity, backed by contemporaneous medical fitness certifications when necessary.
  • Sub-Registrar Office (SRO) Registration: While will registration is not strictly mandatory under Indian law, formally registering the instrument before the jurisdictional Sub-Registrar in Tamil Nadu establishes an undeniable public record and creates a strong legal presumption of genuine execution under Section 51 of the Registration Act, 1908.
Did you know? An unregistered Will is legally valid in India if properly attested, but registering the Will before the Sub-Registrar significantly elevates its evidential standing if disgruntled legal heirs challenge it in civil court.

Probate Petitions & Letters of Administration

A Probate is a formal decree granted under the seal of a competent civil court certifying the validity of a Will and confirming the executor's legal authority to administer the testator's estate.

When is Probate Mandatory in Tamil Nadu?

Under Sections 57 and 213 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925, probate is mandatory for Wills executed by Hindus, Buddhists, Sikhs, or Jains within the historical Presidency Town limits of the Madras High Court (Chennai municipal limits), or where immovable property situated within Chennai municipal limits is bequeathed under the Will.

For properties situated in Thanjavur, Tiruchirappalli, Kumbakonam, Madurai, Coimbatore, and other districts outside Chennai, probate is generally not mandatory under state law. However, obtaining probate or court-issued Letters of Administration is highly recommended or required when:

  • Other legal heirs threaten to challenge the Will on grounds of forgery, fraud, or mental incapacity.
  • Banks, asset management companies, or cooperative housing societies require a court order before releasing high-value assets.
  • No executor was named in the Will (requiring a petition for Letters of Administration with Will Annexed).
  • The deceased passed away without leaving a Will (requiring Letters of Administration upon Intestacy).

Contested Wills & Legal Heir Litigation

Grounds for Challenging a Will in India:

  • Lack of Testamentary Capacity: The testator was physically or mentally incapable of understanding the disposition due to illness, dementia, or coercion.
  • Suspicious Circumstances: Unnatural disposition excluding natural heirs, propounder playing an active part in execution, or signature discrepancies.
  • Fraud, Forgery or Impersonation: Fabricated signatures or fraudulent execution before unregistered witnesses.

Documents Required for Estate Administration

While specific documentation depends on whether succession is testamentary (with a Will) or intestate (without a Will), the following primary documents are typically required:

Original Death Certificate: Issued by the Municipal Corporation, Town Panchayat, or Registrar of Births and Deaths.
Original Will & Codicils: Original signed and attested testamentary instrument (if available).
Parent Title Deeds: Registered Sale Deeds, Partition Deeds, Settlement Deeds, or Gift Deeds in the deceased's name.
Revenue Records (Patta / Chitta): Current extract of Patta and Adangal establishing revenue standing.
Legal Heir Certificate: Tahsildar-issued certificate or genealogical family tree affidavit.
Movable Asset Schedules: Passbooks, fixed deposit receipts, demat account statements, and share certificates.
