Statutory Framework

Understanding SARFAESI Proceedings

An overview of the statutory mechanisms used by secured creditors and the procedural safeguards guaranteed to borrowers, mortgagors, and guarantors.

The Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002 (SARFAESI Act) is a specialised enactment designed to enable banks, housing finance companies, and notified financial institutions to recover secured debts without the prior mandatory requirement of filing a protracted civil suit in ordinary civil courts. When a borrower’s account is classified as a Non-Performing Asset (NPA) in accordance with Reserve Bank of India (RBI) prudential guidelines, the lender acquires statutory enforcement powers against the secured assets.

While the statute provides summary enforcement mechanisms, these powers are strictly conditional upon adherence to statutory procedures codified under Section 13 of the Act and the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules, 2002. Borrowers, guarantors, and property owners facing recovery actions have substantive procedural remedies. Timely, accurate legal counsel is critical because statutory limitation periods under the Act are rigid—often leaving only 60 days to submit representations or 45 days to initiate judicial review before the Debt Recovery Tribunal.

Why Financial Institutions Use It

Unlike ordinary recovery suits that may take years in civil courts, SARFAESI grants secured creditors extrajudicial recovery powers to take symbolic or physical possession of mortgaged properties directly once statutory notices expire.

How Creditors Enforce Security

Enforcement progresses through formal demand notices under Section 13(2), taking possession of the secured asset under Section 13(4), seeking magisterial assistance under Section 14, and conducting public auctions under Rules 8 and 9.

Why Timely Advice is Critical

Failure to respond to statutory notices within stipulated limitation windows can lead to the forfeiture of key procedural objections, physical dispossession, or distress sale of valuable commercial and residential properties.

Procedural Roadmap

Key Stages of SARFAESI Proceedings

A structured breakdown of statutory recovery milestones and corresponding remedy opportunities under the SARFAESI Act, 2002.
01STAGE

Loan Default & NPA Classification

Prudential Guidelines

The borrower defaults on principal or interest repayments. The secured creditor classifies the account as a Non-Performing Asset (NPA) pursuant to Section 2(1)(o) and binding Master Directions issued by the Reserve Bank of India. Valid NPA classification is the jurisdictional prerequisite for initiating all subsequent SARFAESI measures.

02STAGE

Statutory Demand Notice under Section 13(2)

60-Day Cure Period

The secured creditor serves a written demand notice requiring the borrower and guarantors to discharge their liabilities in full within 60 days. The notice must specify the exact amount due, interest calculations, and the secured assets intended to be enforced. During this period, the borrower is statutorily restrained from alienating the secured assets.

03STAGE

Borrower's Representation or Objection under Section 13(3A)

15-Day Bank Response

Within the 60-day window, the borrower has the statutory right to raise written objections challenging the demand, interest computations, or NPA classification. The secured creditor is legally mandated to apply its mind and, if rejecting the objections, communicate reasoned grounds within 15 days. Failure to consider objections creates a significant procedural defect.

04STAGE

Enforcement Measures under Section 13(4) & Section 14

Possession Notice

Upon expiry of the 60-day notice without full repayment, the creditor may take symbolic possession under Rule 8, take over asset management, or apply to the Chief Metropolitan Magistrate (CMM) or District Magistrate (DM) under Section 14 for assistance in taking physical possession. A formal possession notice must be published in two leading newspapers within 7 days.

05STAGE

Application before the DRT under Section 17

45-Day Limitation

Any person aggrieved by Section 13(4) measures—including possession notices, Section 14 magistrate orders, or auction notices—may prefer a Securitisation Application (S.A.) before the jurisdictional Debt Recovery Tribunal (DRT) within 45 days. The DRT examines whether the creditor’s measures comply with statutory law and has the authority to restore possession.

06STAGE

Appellate Review before the DRAT under Section 18

30-Day Window & Pre-Deposit

Any party aggrieved by an order of the DRT may file an appeal before the Debts Recovery Appellate Tribunal (DRAT) within 30 days. Under the second proviso to Section 18(1), no borrower appeal shall be entertained unless 50% of the debt is deposited, which the DRAT may reduce to not less than 25% for reasons recorded in writing.

Statutory Caveat Regarding Process & Remedies: Not every stage described above is mandatory or applicable in every individual case. Procedural steps, limitation computations, and available remedies depend strictly on the underlying security agreements, documentation history, and judicial discretion of competent courts and tribunals.

Understanding Your Legal Remedies

How legal counsel examines compliance with statutory requirements and identifies valid avenues of tribunal review for affected parties.

Review of Demand Notices & Statutory Compliance

Verifying whether the Section 13(2) notice contains itemized principal and interest breakdowns, whether the loan account was correctly classified as an NPA, and whether statutory service requirements were fulfilled.

Scrutiny of Underlying Security & Mortgage Documents

Examining whether valid equitable or registered mortgages were lawfully created, checking registration with the Central Registry of Securitisation Asset Reconstruction and Security Interest of India (CERSAI) under Chapter IV-A, and checking for defects in title deeds.

Examination of Possession & Auction Procedures

Checking adherence to Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules: publication in two newspapers within 7 days under Rule 8(2), affixation of notices, independent valuation by an approved valuer under Rule 8(5), and service of mandatory 30-day sale notices under Rule 8(6).

Securitisation Applications before the DRT

Invoking Section 17 before the Debts Recovery Tribunal to challenge unlawful enforcement measures, seek interlocutory stay orders against dispossession or auction sales, and request the restoration of secured assets.

Statutory Exemptions (Section 31 Exclusions)

Asserting absolute statutory protections: SARFAESI does not apply to agricultural land (Section 31(i)), accounts where the remaining liability is less than 20% of the principal and interest (Section 31(j)), or claims below one lakh rupees (Section 31(h)).

Judicial Fora

Representation Before Debt Recovery Tribunals

Professional legal advocacy across Debt Recovery Tribunals (DRT) and the Debt Recovery Appellate Tribunal (DRAT) in Tamil Nadu.

Debts Recovery Tribunal (DRT)

Tribunal of First Instance — Section 17

The Debts Recovery Tribunal is the dedicated statutory forum having original jurisdiction under Section 17 of the SARFAESI Act. Any aggrieved borrower, guarantor, or third-party owner may file a Securitisation Application (S.A.) within 45 days of any Section 13(4) measure.

The DRT possesses the statutory authority to evaluate whether the secured creditor complied with mandatory rules, grant interlocutory interim stays on property dispossession or auction sales, and direct the restoration of possession if measures are found invalid.

Debts Recovery Appellate Tribunal (DRAT)

Appellate Authority — Section 18

Any party aggrieved by an order passed by the DRT under Section 17 can file a statutory appeal under Section 18 before the Debts Recovery Appellate Tribunal (DRAT) within 30 days of receiving the order.

The DRAT Chennai exercises appellate jurisdiction over DRTs across Tamil Nadu and adjoining southern jurisdictions. Our advocates assist clients with statutory appeal formulation and compliance with mandatory pre-deposit requirements under the second proviso to Section 18(1).

High Court Writ Remedies under Article 226

The Hon’ble Supreme Court of India (in landmark judgments including United Bank of India v. Satyawati Tondon (2010), Celir LLP v. Bafna Motors (2024), and South Indian Bank Ltd. v. Naveen Mathew Philip (2023)) has repeatedly affirmed that because Section 17 of the SARFAESI Act provides an efficacious alternative statutory remedy, High Courts generally do not entertain writ petitions under Article 226 against SARFAESI recovery measures, except in extraordinary circumstances involving total lack of statutory jurisdiction or grave violation of natural justice. RRC Law Associates guides clients toward the legally appropriate forum without recommending frivolous or unmaintainable litigation.

The Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002 (SARFAESI Act) is an Indian statute enacted to enable banks and notified financial institutions to recover secured debts by enforcing security interests against charged assets without the prior mandatory intervention of an ordinary civil court, provided the underlying loan account has been classified as a Non-Performing Asset (NPA).
A notice issued under Section 13(2) is a formal 60-day demand notice informing the borrower and guarantors that the account is classified as an NPA and demanding full discharge of the outstanding debt within 60 days from the date of the notice. During this period, the borrower may either discharge the liability or exercise the statutory right under Section 13(3A) to submit written representations or objections against the demand.
Yes. Under Section 13(3A), the borrower is statutorily entitled to submit a formal written representation or objection to the secured creditor. If the secured creditor rejects the objection, it is statutorily required to communicate the reasons for non-acceptance within 15 days of receipt. However, the proviso to Section 13(3A) clarifies that the rejection of objections does not itself confer an immediate right to approach the DRT at that stage.
If the borrower fails to discharge the liability within the 60-day demand window, the secured creditor may take enforcement measures under Section 13(4), such as taking symbolic or physical possession of the secured assets under Rule 8, taking over asset management, or applying to the Chief Metropolitan Magistrate or District Magistrate under Section 14. Once Section 13(4) measures are initiated, an aggrieved person acquires the statutory right to challenge the action before the DRT under Section 17.

The Debts Recovery Tribunal (DRT) is the specialized tribunal of first instance empowered to examine challenges to bank recovery measures under Section 17. The Debts Recovery Appellate Tribunal (DRAT) is the appellate body established under Section 18 to hear appeals against DRT orders. An appeal to the DRAT requires the borrower to deposit a mandatory pre-deposit of 50% of the debt due, which the DRAT has discretion to reduce to not less than 25% for recorded reasons.
