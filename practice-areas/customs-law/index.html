Frequently Asked Questions — Customs Law & Procedures

Verified legal guidance under the Customs Act, 1962 and appellate jurisprudence
Legal FAQs
Statements recorded under Section 108 of the Customs Act, 1962, are deemed judicial proceedings and are legally admissible evidence in court. The summoned person must promptly consult experienced customs counsel to examine shipping bills, import declarations, and financial records before testifying. If statements are extracted under coercion, threats, or undue duress, a formal retraction application must be submitted immediately to the adjudicating authority and jurisdictional magistrate to preserve evidentiary integrity.
Under Section 110A of the Customs Act, the adjudicating authority holds jurisdiction to provisionally release seized goods pending final adjudication upon the owner executing a bond and furnishing reasonable bank guarantee or cash security. Where provisional release is arbitrarily denied, delayed, or conditioned upon disproportionate security demands, importers can approach the Hon'ble Madras High Court under Article 226 for expeditious release orders to mitigate mounting port demurrage and container detention charges.
Under Section 28(1), the standard statutory limitation period for serving a Show Cause Notice for duty short-levied, not levied, or erroneously refunded is two years from the relevant date. However, under Section 28(4), if the department establishes collusion, willful misstatement, or suppression of facts with intent to evade duty, the limitation period extends to five years. Proving that all material facts were duly disclosed in Bills of Entry and import declarations is the cornerstone of defeating extended limitation demands.

Under Section 125 of the Customs Act, the adjudicating officer holds discretionary jurisdiction to offer the passenger an option to pay a redemption fine in lieu of absolute confiscation, provided the goods are not strictly prohibited under foreign trade legislation. Returning passengers and non-commercial travelers detained at Chennai, Tiruchirappalli, Madurai, or Coimbatore airports can submit formal legal representations seeking redemption, reduction of penalties, and release of detained gold or baggage.
