Taxable Income for NRIs in India – Rental Income, Interest and Investments

An exhaustive legal analysis of the Indian territorial tax net: calculating house property rent, navigating NRO vs. NRE deposit interest, capital gains on mutual funds, Section 195 withholding thresholds, and repatriation under RBI FEMA regulations.

Authored by RRC Law Associates
Published: October 2026
13 min read
Verified for 2026 Compliance
Statutory classification and withholding tax mechanisms on Indian rental income, bank accounts, and investment portfolios.

For non-residents maintaining financial ties to India, the phrase "Indian-source income" carries strict statutory implications. Misconceptions regarding what the Indian Income Tax Department can and cannot tax are widespread among expatriates. Many overseas investors assume that because they reside in the US, UK, Canada, or the Gulf, all their income is governed exclusively by foreign laws.

In truth, Indian tax law operates on a rigorous territorial nexus. Under Section 5(2) and Section 9 of the Income-tax Act, any economic yield that accrues, arises, or is received within the geographical boundaries of India falls squarely into the Indian direct tax net. This guide breaks down the precise tax treatment of rental yields, bank deposits, capital market investments, gifts, and inheritance for overseas Indians.

1. The Territorial Nexus of Indian Taxation: Section 5(2) & Section 9

Under the Indian tax framework (both under the Income-tax Act, 1961 and the incoming Income-tax Act, 2025), Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) enjoy total immunity from Indian taxation on their foreign earnings. Your overseas salary, corporate consulting fees earned abroad, foreign rental properties, and international investment portfolios cannot be touched by the Indian Revenue.

However, under Section 5(2), an NRI's taxable income in India comprises:

  • Income received or deemed to be received in India during the financial year; and
  • Income accruing or arising, or deemed to accrue or arise to the non-resident in India.
Section 9 Deemed Accrual Rules

Under Section 9(1), specific categories of income are statutorily deemed to accrue in India regardless of where the contract is signed or where payment is wired:

  • Income from any business connection or property in India.
  • Income from any asset or source of income situated in India.
  • Capital gains arising from the transfer of any capital asset situated in India.
  • Dividends paid by an Indian domestic company.
  • Interest, royalties, or fees for technical services paid by the Government or an Indian resident.

2. Taxation of Rental Income From Immovable Property in India

One of the most prevalent revenue streams for NRIs is leasing ancestral or purchased residential and commercial properties in Indian metropolitan hubs and heritage towns across Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Maharashtra, and Delhi NCR.

Rental income is taxed under the head "Income from House Property" (Sections 22 to 27). The computational framework provides significant statutory relief:

  1. Gross Annual Value (GAV): Actual annual rent received or contractual rent, whichever is higher.
  2. Less: Municipal Taxes: Local property taxes paid to the municipal corporation or panchayat during the financial year can be deducted, yielding the Net Annual Value (NAV).
  3. Less: Statutory Standard Deduction (Section 24(a)): A flat 30% deduction is allowed on NAV for repairs, maintenance, and upkeep—irrespective of actual expenditure incurred.
  4. Less: Home Loan Interest (Section 24(b)): Full interest paid on a loan taken to acquire or construct the let-out property is deductible (under the Old Tax Regime).
Tenant's Mandatory Section 195 TDS Obligation

Under Section 195, any tenant (corporate or individual) leasing property from an NRI is legally mandated to deduct 30% TDS plus health and education cess (31.2%) from the gross rent before remitting payment.

The tenant must obtain a Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number (TAN) and file Form 27Q quarterly. Because 31.2% withholding often drastically exceeds the NRI's actual taxable liability after the 30% standard deduction, filing an Indian tax return is essential to recover the excess deduction.

3. Taxation of Indian Bank Accounts: NRE, NRO & FCNR Deposits

When an Indian citizen becomes a non-resident under FEMA, maintaining ordinary resident bank accounts is legally prohibited; existing accounts must be converted to Non-Resident Ordinary (NRO) accounts, or new Non-Resident External (NRE) accounts must be opened.

Account Type Funding Currency Interest Taxability Withholding Tax (TDS) Repatriability
NRE Account (Savings/FD) Foreign Currency (Converted to INR) 100% Tax-Free (Sec 10(4)(ii)) Zero TDS Freely Repatriable
FCNR(B) Account (Term Deposit) Designated Foreign Currency (USD, GBP, EUR) 100% Tax-Free (Sec 10(4)(ii)) Zero TDS Freely Repatriable (Zero Forex Risk)
NRO Account (Savings/FD) Indian Rupees (Rent, Dividends, Sale proceeds) Fully Taxable at slab rates 30% + cess (Sec 195) Subject to USD 1M limit & 15CA/CB

Key Takeaway: While NRE and FCNR interest enjoys complete statutory exemption under Section 10(4)(ii), NRO savings interest is subject to automatic 31.2% TDS by the bank, making treaty relief (DTAA) or annual ITR refund claims vital.

4. Capital Gains on Equities, Mutual Funds & Securities

Portfolio investments made by NRIs through the Portfolio Investment Scheme (PIS) or non-PIS NRO/NRE channels are subject to clear capital gains taxation:

  • Listed Equity Shares & Equity Mutual Funds:
    • Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG – held $\le 12$ months): Taxed at 20% under Section 111A (revised under direct tax rationalization).
    • Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG – held $> 12$ months): Gains exceeding INR 1.25 lakhs per financial year are taxed at 12.5% under Section 112A without indexation.
  • Debt Mutual Funds & Fixed Maturity Plans:
    • Investments made after April 1, 2023, are classified as short-term capital gains irrespective of holding period and taxed at applicable individual income tax slab rates under Section 50AA.

Crucial Difference for NRIs: Unlike residents where brokers do not deduct TDS on equity gains, custodians and asset management companies (AMCs) deduct full TDS on NRI capital gains at the time of redemption.

5. Interest on Bonds, Debentures & Government Securities

Overseas investors frequently hold Indian corporate bonds, government securities (G-Secs), and infrastructure debt funds:

  • Infrastructure Debt Funds / Specified Bonds: Subject to concessional withholding rates of 5% plus cess under Section 115A / 194LD (subject to statutory sunset clauses).
  • Corporate Non-Convertible Debentures (NCDs): Interest is taxable at regular slab rates, with standard Section 195 TDS applied at 30% plus cess.
  • Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs): Interest paid semi-annually (2.5% p.a.) is fully taxable at slab rates; however, redemption by an individual at maturity after 8 years is exempt from capital gains tax.

6. Inheritance, Gifts & Transfers from Relatives: Tax Implications

A major area of legal inquiry among expatriates involves receiving inherited wealth, family partition shares, and monetary gifts from aging parents in India.

Statutory Framework on Gifts & Inherited Assets
  • Zero Inheritance Tax: India abolished the Estate Duty Act in 1985. Receiving ancestral land, houses, shares, or jewelry under a registered Will, succession certificate, or legal heirship is 100% tax-free for the recipient.
  • Gifts from Specified Linear Relatives: Under Section 56(2)(x), monetary or immovable gifts received from a "relative" (parents, spouse, siblings, lineal ascendants or descendants) are completely exempt from income tax without monetary ceiling.
  • Gifts from Non-Relatives: Gifts of cash, property, or securities exceeding INR 50,000 in aggregate from friends or non-relatives are classified as "Income from Other Sources" and taxed at normal individual slab rates.

7. The Withholding Tax Framework: Section 195 TDS Compliance

Section 195 of the Income-tax Act places the statutory onus of tax collection on the payer. Any person responsible for remitting sum taxable under the Act to a non-resident must deduct tax at the rates in force.

Because Section 195 does not recognize lower resident thresholds (such as Section 194A's INR 40,000 interest threshold), every single rupee of taxable income paid to an NRI triggers withholding. If a payer fails to deduct tax, they face penal disallowance under Section 40(a)(i) and personal liability under Section 201.

8. Applying Double Taxation Avoidance Agreements (DTAA) for Lower Withholding

Section 90(2) establishes that where the Central Government has entered into a DTAA with a foreign country, the provisions of the Act apply only to the extent they are more beneficial to the non-resident.

NRIs can leverage bilateral treaties to drastically lower domestic withholding rates:

  • India–USA DTAA: Interest on bank deposits capped at 15% (compared to 31.2% domestic).
  • India–UK DTAA: Interest on bank deposits capped at 15%.
  • India–Singapore DTAA: Interest on bank deposits capped at 15%.
  • India–UAE DTAA: Interest on bank deposits capped at 12.5%.

Operational Requirement: To instruct your Indian bank or payer to apply the lower treaty rate, you must furnish your foreign Tax Residency Certificate (TRC), electronically verified Form 10F, and PAN details before the interest or remittance is credited.

9. Repatriating Income Abroad Under RBI FEMA Guidelines (USD 1 Million Scheme)

Once taxes are settled, transferring funds from an NRO account to your overseas bank account in the US, UK, Australia, or UAE is governed by the Foreign Exchange Management (Remittance of Assets) Regulations.

Key rules under the Authorized Dealer (AD Bank) framework:

  1. Annual Ceiling: An NRI or OCI can remit up to USD 1,000,000 (One Million US Dollars) per financial year (April to March) out of balances held in their NRO account arising from legitimate sources (rent, interest, dividends, inheritance, property sales).
  2. Form 15CB (Chartered Accountant Certification): A qualified CA examines the origin of funds, statutory tax calculations, TDS payments, and bank ledgers to certify that all Indian taxes have been paid in full.
  3. Form 15CA (Taxpayer Undertaking): Generated electronically on the Income Tax e-filing portal based on the 15CB certification and submitted to the bank along with FEMA Form A2.

10. Essential Document Checklist for Auditing Indian Income

Income Verification Checklist
  • Form 16A (TDS Certificates): Downloaded from the TRACES portal for rent, bank interest, or AMC capital gains.
  • Annual Information Statement (AIS) / TIS: Comprehensive portal record of high-value cash deposits, interest credits, and stock trades.
  • Registered Lease Agreements & Municipal Receipts: Documenting the exact contractual rental terms and local tax deductions.
  • Bank Form 26AS: Tax credit statement proving payment of TDS by all payers.
  • Foreign Tax Return Records: For cross-reconciliation of foreign tax credits (IRS 1040 / HMRC Self Assessment).
