DTAA Benefits for NRIs: How to Avoid Double Taxation Between India and Your Country of Residence

An authoritative legal examination of Section 90 treaty relief, Tax Residency Certificates (TRC), Form 10F online verification, withholding tax reductions, and foreign tax credits.

Authored by RRC Law Associates
Published: November 2026
14 min read
Verified for 2026 Compliance
Cross-border treaty relief mechanisms, bilateral double taxation agreements, and foreign tax credit optimization for Non-Resident Indians.

Non-Resident Indians may receive income from India while living and working in another country. In certain situations, the same income may also be considered taxable in the country where they currently reside.

When income earned in India—such as bank interest, dividends, royalties, capital gains, or professional fees—is taxed both at source by the Indian Income Tax Department and globally by an overseas tax authority (such as the IRS in the US, HMRC in the UK, or the CRA in Canada), taxpayers face the burden of double taxation. Bilateral Double Taxation Avoidance Agreements (DTAAs) exist specifically to eliminate this overlap through concessional withholding tax rates and foreign tax credits.

1. What Is a Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement?

A Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) is a sovereign bilateral treaty entered into by the Government of India under Section 90 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (and corresponding provisions under Section 159 of the Income-tax Act, 2025) with foreign nations. India currently maintains comprehensive DTAAs with over 85 countries across North America, Europe, Asia, the Middle East, and Australasia.

Principle of Treaty Override: Section 90(2)

Under Section 90(2) of the Income-tax Act, the provisions of the relevant DTAA prevail over domestic income-tax rules to the extent that the treaty provisions are more beneficial to the non-resident taxpayer. This constitutional principle was affirmed by the Supreme Court of India in the landmark ruling Union of India v. Azadi Bachao Andolan.

Consequently, an NRI has the statutory right to choose between the provisions of the Indian domestic tax law and the applicable bilateral treaty, whichever results in lower tax liability.

2. Why Are DTAAs Important for NRIs?

For overseas Indians managing financial assets, ancestral estates, or investments across borders, invoking DTAA protections delivers substantial practical advantages:

  • Substantial Withholding Tax Reductions: Domestic Indian withholding tax (TDS) under Section 195 on income paid to non-residents is deducted at standard rates of 20% to 30% plus surcharge and cess. DTAAs commonly reduce this rate to 10%–15%.
  • Prevention of Juridical Double Taxation: Treaties establish clear jurisdictional boundaries deciding which country has the primary right to tax a specific category of income.
  • Foreign Tax Credits (FTC): Where income is taxed in India, the DTAA guarantees that the taxpayer can claim a tax credit in their home country, offsetting domestic tax liabilities dollar-for-dollar.
  • Dispute Resolution via Mutual Agreement Procedure (MAP): Bilateral agreements provide formal inter-governmental mechanisms to resolve complex jurisdictional conflicts and prevent double taxation.

3. Which Income Categories May Be Covered?

DTAAs allocate taxing rights across specific articles corresponding to the source and nature of income:

Income Stream Domestic Indian TDS (Sec 195) Typical DTAA Concessional Rate Primary Taxing Jurisdiction
NRO Savings & FD Interest 30% + Surcharge & Cess 10% to 15% (e.g., USA, UK, UAE, Singapore) Source Country (India capped rate)
Dividend Income 20% + Surcharge & Cess 10% to 15% Shared (Source capped, Residence credit)
Royalties & Fees for Technical Services (FTS) 20% + Surcharge & Cess 10% to 15% Source Country (with treaty rate cap)
Rental Income from Indian Immovable Property 30% + Surcharge & Cess Normal domestic rates (taxed in India) Source Country (India has primary right)
Capital Gains on Indian Real Estate 12.5% to 20% + Cess Taxed in India under Article 13 Source Country (India has primary right)

Note: Real estate rental and capital gains are almost universally taxable in the source country under Article 6 and Article 13 of OECD/UN model treaties. Double taxation on real estate is eliminated via foreign tax credit in your country of residence, not by domestic Indian tax exemption.

4. What Is a Tax Residency Certificate?

A Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) is an official document issued by the revenue authority of the country where the NRI resides, confirming that the individual is a tax resident of that country under its domestic tax statutes.

Mandatory Statutory Requirement: Section 90(4)

Under Section 90(4) of the Income-tax Act, 1961, an NRI is strictly legally disentitled from claiming treaty relief unless they obtain a valid TRC from the foreign government. Neither a passport, foreign visa, green card, overseas driver license, nor utility bill can substitute for a statutory TRC.

Common issuing authorities include:

  • United States: Internal Revenue Service (IRS) — Form 6166 (Certification of U.S. Tax Residency).
  • United Kingdom: HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) — Certificate of Residence.
  • United Arab Emirates: Federal Tax Authority (FTA) — Tax Residency Certificate.
  • Singapore: Inland Revenue Authority of Singapore (IRAS) — Certificate of Residence.
  • Canada: Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) — Certificate of Residency.
  • Australia: Australian Taxation Office (ATO) — Certificate of Residency.

5. What Documents May Be Required to Claim DTAA Benefits?

To enable an Indian deductor (such as a bank, tenant, or remitter) to apply lower treaty withholding rates, or to claim treaty relief on an Indian income tax return, an NRI must assemble the following documentation dossier:

  1. Valid Tax Residency Certificate (TRC): Covering the relevant Indian financial year (1 April to 31 March).
  2. Form 10F (Electronically Verified): Under Section 90(5) and Rule 21AB, if the overseas TRC does not contain all prescribed details (status, nationality, tax identification number, period of validity, overseas address), Form 10F must be generated and digitally verified on the official Income Tax portal.
  3. Permanent Account Number (PAN): Valid Indian PAN linked with Aadhaar (or exempted status for non-residents).
  4. No Permanent Establishment (PE) & Beneficial Ownership Declaration: A formal signed self-declaration confirming that the NRI has no business establishment or fixed base in India through which the income is generated, and that they are the sole beneficial owner of the funds.
  5. Passport & Overseas Address Proof: Verifying non-resident travel history and foreign domicile.

6. How Does DTAA Relief Work?

International tax treaties employ two recognized methods to resolve overlapping tax claims:

A. The Exemption Method

The taxpayer's country of residence completely exempts specific categories of income that have been taxed in India. This method is common in certain European jurisdictions.

B. The Tax Credit Method (FTC)

The residence country includes Indian income in the taxpayer's global return, but grants a credit for taxes paid in India against the foreign liability (e.g., US IRS Form 1116 or UK HMRC Foreign Tax Credit Relief).

Claiming Treaty Relief at Source vs. Through Return Filing

There are two procedural routes to access DTAA benefits:

  • Route 1: Prior Submission to Deductor: By submitting the TRC, Form 10F, and self-declaration to your Indian bank before interest is credited, the bank directly withholds TDS at the lower treaty rate (e.g., 15% instead of 30%).
  • Route 2: Claiming Refund via ITR-2: If the bank or remitter deducted the full 30% domestic rate because documents were not provided in advance, the NRI can claim DTAA relief under Section 90 when filing their annual return on the e-filing portal, receiving a direct refund of the excess tax.

7. Important Treaty Compliance Changes From 1 April 2026

Cross-border compliance is undergoing structural modernization under Indian direct tax statutes:

Prospective Application of the Income-tax Act, 2025

The Income-tax Act, 2025 governs tax years beginning on or after 1 April 2026. Earlier financial years continue under the 1961 Act. Treaty provisions are harmonized under Section 159 of the 2025 Act, with transitional preservation of existing bilateral pacts under Section 395(1).

Furthermore, the implementation of the OECD Multilateral Instrument (MLI) incorporates the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) into covered tax treaties. Non-residents must demonstrate genuine economic nexus and bona fide residency in the treaty partner jurisdiction.

8. Common DTAA Mistakes NRIs Should Avoid

In cross-border tax advisory, we regularly encounter these recurring pitfalls:

  1. Assuming DTAA Applies Automatically: Banks and deductors are statutorily mandated to deduct at peak domestic rates (30% + cess) unless a complete TRC and Form 10F dossier is officially on file prior to deduction.
  2. Confusing NRE Interest with NRO Interest: NRE deposit interest is already 100% tax-free under domestic Section 10(4)(ii). DTAA rates apply to reduce tax on NRO interest, royalties, and dividends.
  3. Believing Real Estate in India Is Exempt: Assuming that living in the US or UK exempts you from paying tax on Indian rental income or property capital gains. Treaties explicitly reserve real estate taxation to the source country.
  4. Delaying TRC Procurement: Overseas tax revenue bodies often take 4 to 8 weeks to issue a TRC. Delaying the application until the end of the financial year results in missed deduction deadlines.
  5. Failing to Report Foreign Tax Credit Abroad: Paying tax in India without claiming FTC on foreign returns, resulting in unnecessary out-of-pocket double taxation.
