Statutory Classification

The MSMED Act 2006 & The Unified Composite Criteria

The Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development (MSMED) Act, 2006 provides the primary legislative architecture governing small enterprise empowerment, credit access, and dispute adjudication across India. Through Notification S.O. 2119(E), the Ministry of MSME introduced a unified composite classification system eliminating earlier distinctions between manufacturing and services.

Crucially, under the amended statutory regime, all export turnover of goods and services is fully excluded from the turnover calculation, allowing export-oriented firms to retain their Micro or Small status and continue enjoying extensive statutory subsidies.

Category Plant & Equipment Annual Turnover

Special Statutory Exclusion: As per Paragraph 4 of Notification 2119(E), exports of goods or services or both are not reckoned for calculating turnover for any enterprise category.

Required Information & Documents for Udyam

Under the modern paperless registration regime, no physical documents need to be uploaded, but statutory data must precisely match official registries.

Identity & Signatory

  • Aadhaar Number of Authorized Signatory / Proprietor / Managing Partner
  • Permanent Account Number (PAN) of Enterprise or Proprietor
  • Active Mobile Number linked with Aadhaar for OTP authentication
  • Email address of the enterprise for statutory correspondence
  • Social category (General, SC, ST, OBC) & Gender of primary promoter

Enterprise Details

  • Official Legal Name of Enterprise matching PAN records
  • Form of Organisation (Proprietorship, Partnership, Pvt Ltd, LLP)
  • Full physical address of Principal Place & Manufacturing Units
  • Date of Enterprise Incorporation / Commencement of Business
  • National Industrial Classification (NIC) primary and secondary codes

Banking & Financials

  • Active Enterprise Current Bank Account Number & IFSC Code
  • Goods & Services Tax Identification Number (GSTIN), if statutorily applicable
  • Written Down Value (WDV) of Plant & Machinery from latest ITR
  • Gross Domestic Turnover and Export Turnover figures
  • Total count of employees (Male, Female, Others)

Statutory Debt Enforcement

Delayed Payment Recovery Legal Desk (MSMED Act)

Unpaid MSME suppliers enjoy unprecedented statutory remedies under Chapter V of the MSMED Act, overriding standard contract law.

Mandatory 45-Day Cap

Under Section 15 of the MSMED Act, a buyer must make payment within the period agreed in writing, which cannot under any circumstances exceed 45 days from the date of acceptance or deemed acceptance of goods or services.

3x RBI Penal Interest

Under Section 16, default in payment beyond the statutory period automatically incurs mandatory compound interest with monthly rests at three times the Bank Rate notified by the Reserve Bank of India. This interest cannot be waived by agreement.

MSEFC Arbitration & Samadhaan

Under Section 18, unpaid suppliers can initiate dispute resolution before the Micro and Small Enterprise Facilitation Council (MSEFC) via the MSME Samadhaan portal. Decisions take the form of enforceable arbitral awards.

Frequently Asked Questions: MSME & Udyam Registration

Authoritative legal answers regarding enterprise classification, IP fee waivers, and delayed payment enforcement.

Under Ministry of MSME Notification S.O. 2119(E) dated 26th June 2020, classification is based on a composite criteria of Investment in Plant & Machinery/Equipment and Annual Turnover: (1) Micro Enterprise: Investment up to INR 1 Crore AND Turnover up to INR 5 Crores; (2) Small Enterprise: Investment up to INR 10 Crores AND Turnover up to INR 50 Crores; (3) Medium Enterprise: Investment up to INR 50 Crores AND Turnover up to INR 250 Crores. Crucially, export turnover of goods or services is completely excluded from the turnover calculation.

Under Chapter V (Sections 15 to 24) of the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development (MSMED) Act 2006, a buyer must make payment to a registered MSME supplier within the agreed date, which cannot legally exceed 45 days. In the event of default, Section 16 mandates payment of compound interest with monthly rests at 3 times the Bank Rate notified by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). Disputes can be referred to the Micro and Small Enterprise Facilitation Council (MSEFC) through the MSME Samadhaan portal.

Under the Trade Marks Rules 2017 and Patent Rules 2003, entities holding a valid Udyam Registration Certificate qualify as 'Small Enterprises' eligible for statutory fee concessions: Trademark application (Form TM-A) official filing fees are reduced by 50% (from INR 9,000 to INR 4,500), and official patent filing fees are rebated by 80%, providing massive capital savings for growing innovators.

As per the Central Government guidelines, having a PAN is mandatory for all applicants. A GSTIN is required only for entities that are statutorily liable to obtain GST registration under the Central Goods and Services Tax Act 2017. Enterprises that are exempt from GST registration (such as those below statutory turnover thresholds or dealing exclusively in exempt items) can obtain Udyam registration using PAN alone.

Registered MSMEs are entitled to tender sets free of cost, complete exemption from payment of Earnest Money Deposit (EMD), and price preference in competitive bidding.
