Visual Monopoly Rights

Protecting Eye-Appeal & Product Aesthetics

In competitive retail and industrial commerce, consumer purchasing decisions are heavily driven by visual shape, styling, and ergonomic contours. Without formal industrial design registration, competitors can easily copy your product exterior while altering internal mechanics, free from patent infringement.

Governed by the Designs Act, 2000 and the Designs Rules, 2001, registration protects the features of shape, configuration, pattern, ornament, or composition of lines or colours applied to any article in two or three-dimensional form. A registered design grants legal monopoly for up to 15 years (initial 10 years + 5-year extension).

Statutory Mandate

Statutory Rigor Under the Designs Act, 2000

The protection of industrial designs is centrally managed by the Patent Office (Design Wing) in Kolkata, with nationwide legal jurisdiction across India.

  • Section 2(d): Defines design as only the features of shape, configuration, pattern, ornament applied to any article by an industrial process, but excludes any mode of construction or mechanical device.
  • Section 11: Establishes copyright in registered designs for an initial term of 10 years, extendable to 15 years upon payment of extension fees.
  • Section 22: Grants powerful civil enforcement against piracy, awarding statutory sums up to ₹50,000 per violation and injunctions against retail distribution.

The "Judged Solely by the Eye" Test

Indian courts apply the historic "eye appeal" doctrine. In an infringement dispute, the judge assesses whether the allegedly infringing product creates an identical or substantially similar visual impression on the eye of an ordinary customer.

Minor, immaterial modifications will not shield an infringer from liability under Section 22 if the substantial aesthetic novelty of the registered design has been appropriated.

Important Notice: Commercial marketing or public disclosure prior to filing invalidates design novelty under Section 4.

International Client Support

Paris Convention Priority & Remote NRI Filing

Foreign consumer electronics firms, international packaging manufacturers, and overseas Indian product designers can secure statutory design protection in India without physical travel.

Under Section 44 of the Designs Act and the Paris Convention, foreign applicants can claim priority in India within 6 months from the date of filing in their home jurisdiction (e.g., USPTO, EUIPO, UKIPO).

  • 6-Month Paris Convention priority window management.

Territoriality Notice

An Indian Industrial Design registration provides legal exclusivity strictly within India. It does not automatically grant international protection. Separate convention filings must be initiated within the 6-month priority period in other jurisdictions.

Clarifications & Guidance

Frequently Asked Questions

Statutory answers regarding industrial design protection, eligibility, and enforcement.

A Patent protects the functional, mechanical, or technological utility of how an invention works under the Patents Act, 1970. An Industrial Design protects strictly the non-functional visual aesthetic appearance, shape, configuration, pattern, or ornament of an article judged solely by the eye under the Designs Act, 2000. Features dictated solely by mechanical function are excluded from design registration.

Under Section 11 of the Designs Act, 2000, registration initially grants statutory copyright in the design for 10 years from the date of registration. Before the expiry of this 10-year period, the proprietor can file Form 3 with the statutory extension fee to extend protection for an additional 5-year term, yielding a maximum total protection period of 15 years.

Under Section 4, a design must: (1) Be new or original; (2) Not have been disclosed to the public anywhere in India or abroad by prior publication or use prior to filing; (3) Be significantly distinguishable from known designs or combinations thereof; and (4) Not contain scandalous or obscene matter.

Under the Design Rules 2001, the application must include standardized orthographic perspective views showing the article from all angles: Front, Rear, Left Side, Right Side, Top Plan, Bottom Plan, and Isometric/Perspective view. Drawings must include explicit Statement of Novelty disclaimers.

The Locarno Classification is an international classification framework comprising 32 classes and multiple subclasses used to categorize industrial designs based on the nature of the manufactured articles (e.g., Class 6 for furniture, Class 9 for packaging containers, Class 12 for transport, Class 14 for telecommunication equipment).

Section 22 of the Designs Act classifies unauthorized commercial reproduction, imitation, or importation of a registered design as 'Piracy of Registered Design'. The registered proprietor can institute civil suits before the District Court or High Court to recover statutory damages up to ₹50,000 per contravention, seek actual damages, and obtain temporary/permanent injunctions.
