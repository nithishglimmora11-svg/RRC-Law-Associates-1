Who Can Adopt a Child in India? Eligibility for Couples and Single Parents

A definitive legal guide examining statutory eligibility criteria under Section 57 of the Juvenile Justice Act and Regulation 5 of Adoption Regulations 2022, covering composite age limits, single parent restrictions, financial benchmarks, and personal law rules.

Last Updated: 21 November 2026 13 Min Read Statutory Reference: Reg. 5 Adoption Regulations, 2022

Essential Eligibility Takeaways

Minimum Age Threshold: Prospective Adoptive Parents (PAPs) must be at least 25 years old. A minimum age difference of 25 years between the child and either adoptive parent is mandatory (relaxations apply to relative/step-parent adoptions).

Composite Age Limits for Couples: The cumulative age of both spouses determines eligibility for child age brackets: up to 85 years for infants below 2; up to 90 years for children 2–4; up to 100 years for children 4–8; and up to 110 years for children 8–18.

Single Female vs Single Male: A single woman can adopt a child of ANY gender. However, under Section 57(4) of the JJ Act, a single male applicant is strictly prohibited from adopting a female child.

Existing Children Cap: Couples with two or more living biological or adopted children are generally restricted from adopting normal healthy children; they may only adopt children with special needs, hard-to-place children, or relative/step-children.

1. Introduction to Adoption Eligibility in India

The desire to become a parent is one of the most fundamental human aspirations. Under Indian law, however, adoption is not an unfettered legal entitlement of prospective parents; rather, it is a strictly regulated child welfare mechanism designed to guarantee that vulnerable children are placed into stable, loving, and legally protective homes.

Statutory eligibility criteria in India are anchored primarily in Section 57 of the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015, and further operationalized by Regulation 5 of the Adoption Regulations, 2022. In parallel, for Hindus seeking direct family adoptions, Sections 7, 8, and 11 of the Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956 (HAMA) provide personal law qualifications. Navigating these requirements demands a precise understanding of the statutory framework before submitting an application on the government CARINGS portal or executing an adoption deed.

2. Adoption Eligibility Under the Juvenile Justice Framework

The Juvenile Justice Act, 2015, establishes a secular adoption regime open to all Indian citizens, Non-Resident Indians (NRIs), Overseas Citizens of India (OCIs), and qualified foreign nationals regardless of their religious faith. Regulation 5 of the Adoption Regulations, 2022, sets forth five foundational prerequisites that every prospective parent must satisfy:

  • Physical Fitness: The prospective adoptive parents must be physically healthy and free from contagious, terminal, or debilitating medical conditions that could compromise long-term caregiving.
  • Mental & Emotional Alertness: Applicants must be emotionally mature, psychologically stable, and evaluated as capable of providing a nurturing, secure environment.
  • Financial Capability: PAPs must demonstrate stable income and financial means sufficient to support the child's living, healthcare, and educational needs.
  • Composite Age Compliance: Applicants must meet the statutory age tiers linked directly to the age preference of the child at the time of registration.
  • Clean Legal Background: Prospective parents must have no history of child abuse, domestic violence, moral turpitude, or criminal convictions involving harm to minors.

3. Eligibility of Married Couples

For married couples seeking joint adoption under Section 57(2) of the JJ Act, the law imposes strict structural and consent criteria:

  • Marital Stability: The couple must have at least two years of stable marital relationship prior to the date of application. This requirement is verified through the registered marriage certificate. The two-year stability requirement is waived in relative adoptions and step-parent adoptions.
  • Mandatory Joint Consent: Adoption cannot be unilateral. Both husband and wife must give written, unequivocal consent to the adoption. If either spouse withholds consent, the adoption cannot proceed under any circumstances.
  • Composite Age Test: The couple's eligibility for a child is calculated based on their cumulative (composite) age on the date of CARINGS registration.

Statutory Excerpt: Section 57(2) of the JJ Act, 2015

"A married couple can adopt with the consent of both the spouses, and no child shall be given in adoption to a couple unless they have at least two years of stable marital relationship."

4. Eligibility of Single Female Applicants

Indian adoption law is notably progressive regarding single female applicants. Under Section 57(3) of the JJ Act and Regulation 5(2)(b) of Adoption Regulations, 2022:

  • Gender Flexibility: A single female prospective parent is legally entitled to adopt a child of any gender—either a male child (boy) or a female child (girl).
  • Marital Status Coverage: "Single female" includes unmarried women, legally divorced women, and widowed women.
  • Divorce Verification: A divorced applicant must provide a certified copy of the final divorce decree showing no pending appellate challenges.
  • Support Ecosystem: Single mothers must demonstrate a robust family and social support network (e.g., parents, siblings, or trusted guardians) who can provide care in case of emergency.

5. Eligibility of Single Male Applicants

While Indian law allows single male applicants to adopt, it enforces a strict gender-based statutory prohibition:

  • Male Child Only: Under Section 57(4) of the JJ Act and Regulation 5(2)(c) of Adoption Regulations 2022, a single male applicant can only adopt a male child.
  • Absolute Bar on Adopting Female Children: A single male is strictly barred by statute from adopting a female child. This prohibition applies across all institutional, relative, and step-parent adoptions.
  • Policy Justification: The legislative intent behind Section 57(4) is rooted in child protection policies designed to safeguard vulnerable minor girls from potential abuse, reflecting the protective doctrine of parens patriae.

Strict Statutory Prohibition: Section 57(4) JJ Act, 2015

"A single male is not eligible to adopt a girl child." No court, District Magistrate, or administrative authority has the discretion to waive this statutory bar.

6. Age Requirements and Composite Age Brackets

Under Regulation 5(4) of the Adoption Regulations, 2022, the maximum permissible age of prospective adoptive parents is calibrated strictly according to the age of the child at the time of CARINGS registration. This statutory matrix ensures that parents have the physical vigor to raise the child through maturity.

Child Age Category Max Composite Age (Married Couple) Max Individual Age (Either Spouse) Max Age (Single Parent)
Up to 2 years (Infant) 85 years cumulative 55 years individual 40 years
Older than 2 up to 4 years 90 years cumulative 55 years individual 45 years
Older than 4 up to 8 years 100 years cumulative 55 years individual 50 years
Older than 8 up to 18 years 110 years cumulative 55 years individual 55 years

Key Age Rules to Note:

  • Age Calculation Date: The age of prospective adoptive parents is determined as on the date of their online registration on the CARINGS portal.
  • 25-Year Age Gap: The age difference between the child and either prospective adoptive parent must not be less than 25 years. This rule does not apply to relative or step-parent adoptions.
  • Special Needs Relaxations: Age criteria may be relaxed by CARA for prospective parents who volunteer to adopt children with special medical needs, hard-to-place children, or sibling groups.

7. Physical, Mental, Emotional and Financial Suitability

Eligibility is not established solely through paperwork; it is subject to rigorous socio-psychological evaluation during the Home Study Report (HSR):

  • Medical Examination: Both prospective parents must undergo a comprehensive medical examination by a government-registered medical officer (MBBS minimum). The medical report must verify absence of chronic, contagious, or terminal illnesses (such as advanced cancer, active hepatitis, or uncontrolled psychiatric conditions).
  • Psychological Stability: The assessing social worker evaluates emotional resilience, motivation for adoption, marital harmony, and attitudes toward parenting non-biological children.
  • Financial Solvency: While Indian law does not prescribe a rigid minimum wealth threshold, PAPs must demonstrate steady, legal income through Income Tax Returns (ITR), salary slips, audited balance sheets, or property holdings. The financial benchmark ensures the child will not face deprivation.

8. Consent Requirements

Consent is the foundational pillar of legal adoption. In the absence of valid statutory consent, an adoption order cannot be granted:

  • Spousal Consent: In joint applications, both spouses must execute written consent affidavits. A spouse cannot apply unilaterally while remaining married, even if living separately, unless legally divorced.
  • Child's Consent (Above 5 Years): Under Regulation 5(5) of Adoption Regulations 2022, if the child being adopted is five years of age or older, the child's written consent and views must be ascertained and recorded by the adoption agency.
  • Biological Parent Consent (Relative / Step-Parent Adoption): In relative and step-parent adoptions under Chapters V and VI of the Regulations, biological parents must execute formal consent forms (Schedule XX). In step-parent adoption, the consent of the non-custodial biological parent is mandatory unless dispensed with by court order due to death or legal forfeiture.

9. Existing Children and Child Caps

Under Regulation 5(6) of Adoption Regulations 2022, the presence of existing children in the prospective family imposes statutory restrictions:

  • Couples with Two or More Children: Prospective adoptive parents who have two or more children (whether biological or previously adopted) are legally restricted from adopting a normal, healthy infant.
  • Exceptions for Special Needs & Siblings: Couples with two or more existing children may only adopt children classified as having special needs (as per Schedule XVIII), children who are older or hard-to-place, or in cases of relative and step-parent adoptions.
  • Child Welfare Rationale: This statutory cap ensures that orphaned, abandoned, and surrendered infants without families are prioritized for placement with childless couples or families with only one child.

10. Eligibility Under Hindu Law (HAMA 1956)

For Hindus, Buddhists, Jains, and Sikhs adopting directly within the family under the Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956, separate personal law eligibility rules apply:

  • Capacity of Male Hindu (Section 7): Must be of sound mind and not a minor. If married, he must obtain the consent of his wife unless she has renounced the world, ceased to be a Hindu, or been declared of unsound mind by a competent civil court.
  • Capacity of Female Hindu (Section 8): Following the Personal Laws (Amendment) Act, 2010, an unmarried woman, widow, divorcee, or a married woman whose husband is disqualified has full legal capacity to adopt in her own right.
  • Same-Sex Living Child Bar (Section 11(i) & (ii)):
    • If adopting a son: The adoptive parents must not have a living Hindu son, grandson (son's son), or great-grandson.
    • If adopting a daughter: The adoptive parents must not have a living Hindu daughter or granddaughter (daughter's daughter).
  • 21-Year Age Difference (Section 11(iii) & (iv)): If adopting a child of the opposite sex, the adoptive parent must be at least 21 years older than the child being adopted.

For exhaustive analysis of HAMA statutory rules, read our dedicated guide on Adoption Under Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956.

11. Documents Required to Establish Eligibility

To satisfy CARA verification and the District Magistrate's scrutiny, prospective parents must furnish the following authenticated documents:

Current joint family photograph of prospective adoptive parents
PAN Card and Aadhaar Card / Passport for identity and address proof
Birth certificates or Matriculation certificates proving age of PAPs
Marriage certificate or divorce decree / death certificate of former spouse
Medical fitness certificate issued by an MBBS doctor in prescribed format
Three years Income Tax Returns (ITR), salary slips, and employment proof
Proof of residence: Registered sale deed, electricity bill, or rent agreement
Written consent of existing children aged above 5 years in the household

13. Conclusion

Meeting adoption eligibility in India requires methodical preparation, statutory compliance, and meticulous documentation. Understanding whether you qualify as a married couple, single parent, or relative adopter under the Juvenile Justice framework or HAMA ensures that your application proceeds without administrative rejection. Consulting an experienced family law advocate before filing on CARINGS or executing an adoption deed guarantees that your parental rights are legally secure and unchallengeable.
