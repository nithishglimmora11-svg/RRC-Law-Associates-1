[29] (style=NormalWeb, num=False) Buying, inheriting or selling property in India can raise important tax and compliance questions for Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) and Overseas Citizens of India (OCI) cardholders.
[30] (style=NormalWeb, num=False) An overseas property owner may need to determine whether capital gains tax applies, whether the buyer must deduct tax at source, how the cost of inherited property is calculated and whether sale proceeds can be transferred abroad.
[31] (style=NormalWeb, num=False) These questions become particularly important when the property was inherited from a parent or grandparent, purchased many years ago, or jointly owned by several family members.
[32] (style=NormalWeb, num=False) Indian property transactions can involve three distinct legal questions: the tax payable on the transaction, compliance with foreign-exchange regulations and the conditions governing remittance of money outside India. Satisfying one requirement does not automatically satisfy the others.
[33] (style=NormalWeb, num=False) This guide explains the principal considerations for NRIs and OCI cardholders dealing with property in India.

[52] (style=Heading2, num=False) 2. What Is TDS When an NRI Sells Property in India?
[53] (style=NormalWeb, num=False) Tax deducted at source, commonly called TDS, is a mechanism through which tax is deducted at the time of a specified payment and deposited with the government.
[54] (style=NormalWeb, num=False) When property is sold by a non-resident, the buyer may have a withholding obligation under the applicable income-tax legislation.
[55] (style=NormalWeb, num=False) This is an important difference from a property transaction involving a resident seller, where a different withholding framework may apply.
[56] (style=Heading3, num=False) Why does the distinction matter?
[57] (style=NormalWeb, num=False) A buyer who assumes that the rules applicable to a resident seller also apply to a non-resident seller may calculate the deduction incorrectly.
[58] (style=NormalWeb, num=False) The applicable withholding treatment may depend on:
[59] (style=NormalWeb, num=True) Whether the seller is resident or non-resident for the relevant tax provisions.
[60] (style=NormalWeb, num=True) The nature of the asset.
[61] (style=NormalWeb, num=True) The amount and character of the gain.
[62] (style=NormalWeb, num=True) The date of transfer.
[63] (style=NormalWeb, num=True) The applicable statutory rates and additional charges.
[64] (style=NormalWeb, num=True) Any relevant certificate or order issued by the tax authority.
[65] (style=NormalWeb, num=False) The buyer should not determine the withholding obligation solely from the seller's citizenship or the fact that the seller holds an OCI card.
[66] (style=Heading3, num=False) Is TDS the same as the final tax payable?
[67] (style=NormalWeb, num=False) No. TDS is a withholding mechanism. The final tax liability must be determined under the applicable tax law.
[68] (style=NormalWeb, num=False) Depending on the circumstances, the amount deducted may be higher or lower than the final liability determined after considering the applicable computation, deductions, exemptions and other provisions.
[69] (style=NormalWeb, num=False) The seller should retain the relevant transaction and withholding records and obtain professional advice on the appropriate return-filing and tax-compliance steps.
