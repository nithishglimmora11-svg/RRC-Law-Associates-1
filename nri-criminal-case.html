Detailed Criminal Legal Services for Non-Resident Indians

We represent overseas clients in both prospective complaints registered under new enactments and pending matters governed by the statutory savings framework:

High Court FIR Quashing & Abuse of Process Relief

When an FIR is registered with oblique motives—such as coercing a property settlement, extorting financial concessions, or pressuring an NRI during matrimonial discord—the High Court holds inherent statutory powers under Section 528 of the BNSS (earlier Section 482 CrPC) to prevent the abuse of the judicial process. In accordance with the landmark principles articulated by the Supreme Court in State of Haryana v. Bhajan Lal, our criminal defense advocates draft comprehensive quashing petitions before the Principal Seat or Madurai Bench of the Madras High Court to establish that the uncontroverted allegations do not disclose any cognizable offence.

Where disputes are purely civil or commercial in nature, we demonstrate that the complainant has improperly invoked the criminal justice machinery to bypass civil litigation, seeking quashing or interim stay of police investigation.

BNSS Sec. 528 Inherent Powers CrPC Sec. 482 (Pending Savings) Bhajan Lal Supreme Court Principles Related: NRI Property Disputes

Anticipatory Bail & Pre-Arrest Protection for Traveling NRIs

An NRI learning of a criminal complaint faces the immediate threat of custodial arrest upon landing at an Indian airport (such as Chennai, Trichy, Coimbatore, or Madurai). Under Section 482 of the BNSS (earlier Section 438 CrPC), an individual apprehending arrest may seek an Anticipatory Bail order from the jurisdictional Sessions Court or High Court. It is essential to understand that anticipatory bail is not an absolute immunity from prosecution; it is a judicial direction ordering the investigating agency to release the applicant on bail in the event of arrest, subject to court-imposed conditions.

Courts frequently impose specific conditions on overseas applicants, such as providing local solvent sureties (typically blood relatives in Tamil Nadu), surrendering passport or seeking trial court permission prior to leaving India, and joining the investigation. We assist clients in presenting strong applications that demonstrate established overseas employment, roots in the community, and absence of flight risk.

BNSS Sec. 482 Pre-Arrest Bail CrPC Sec. 438 (Pending Cases) Local Surety & Compliance Guidance

Lookout Circular (LOC) Technical Relief & Passport Rights

A Lookout Circular (LOC) is an administrative alert requested by an originating agency (police, CBI, or financial authority) and maintained by the Bureau of Immigration. If an LOC has been opened against an NRI, immigration authorities will detain or intercept the individual at any Indian port of entry or departure. Where an LOC has been opened arbitrarily—such as in private civil disputes or without adhering to Ministry of Home Affairs guidelines—we file Writ Petitions under Article 226 of the Constitution before the High Court seeking its judicial quashing or interim permission to travel.

Similarly, under Section 10(3)(e) & 10(3)(h) of the Passports Act, 1967, passport offices and Indian consulates abroad may place a hold on passport re-issuance due to a pending criminal case. We move applications before the competent magistrate court under Notification GSR 570(E) to secure judicial permission for passport renewal for a defined duration.

Article 226 High Court Writs Passports Act Sec. 10(3) GSR 570(E) Passport Renewal Petitions

BNS & BNSS Compliance: Dual Statutory Framework for NRIs

With the enactment of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), and Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) effective from 1 July 2024, navigating criminal defense in India requires strict statutory differentiation. Under Section 531(2)(a) of the BNSS and Section 358 of the BNS, all investigations, trials, and inquiries pending prior to 1 July 2024 are governed exclusively by the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 and Indian Penal Code, 1860. Conversely, new complaints registered for post-July 2024 incidents follow the BNS and BNSS.

We assist overseas clients across key offence categories, including cheating (Section 318 BNS / Section 420 IPC), criminal breach of trust (Section 316 BNS / Section 406 IPC), forged land documents (Sections 336 & 340 BNS / Sections 467 & 471 IPC), and marital cruelty allegations (Section 85 BNS / Section 498A IPC). We ensure statutory compliance, accurate legal drafting, and rigorous defense before competent courts in Tamil Nadu.

BNSS Sec. 531 Savings Clause BNS Penal Enactments BSA Electronic Evidence Standards Related: NRI Power of Attorney

Summons, Warrants, Exemption from Appearance & Proclamation Defense

A common crisis for overseas Indians is receiving summons or non-bailable warrants (NBWs) when they are unable to return immediately to India. If unaddressed, trial courts may initiate proclamation proceedings under Section 84 of the BNSS (earlier Section 82 CrPC) declaring the person a Proclaimed Offender, followed by property attachment under Section 85 BNSS (earlier Section 83 CrPC). Additionally, under the newly introduced Section 356 of the BNSS, where an accused declared as a proclaimed offender has absconded to evade trial, the court may proceed with the trial and record evidence in absentia under strictly defined statutory conditions.

Our advocates represent overseas clients by filing applications to recall NBWs, proving lack of valid overseas service under Ministry of Home Affairs comprehensive guidelines, and moving petitions under Section 228 BNSS (earlier Section 205 CrPC) or Section 355 BNSS (earlier Section 317 CrPC) to dispense with personal attendance during routine trial proceedings.

BNSS Sec. 228 & 355 (Personal Exemption) BNSS Sec. 84 & 85 (Attachment Defense) BNSS Sec. 356 (Trial in Absentia Rules) NBW Recall Applications

  • Charge sheet or final report, if filed
  • Previous bail orders, if any

Identity & Overseas Records

  • Current and previous passport copies
  • Immigration entry and exit stamps

Case Supporting Evidence

Legal Authorization

  • Executed Vakalatnama for advocate
  • Special Power of Attorney, if applicable
  • Consular or Apostille attestation proof
  • Government-issued photo identification
  • Local contact & surety documentation

Under Section 482 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS) or earlier Section 438 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 (CrPC), an individual who reasonably apprehends arrest in a non-bailable offence may file an application for anticipatory bail through their advocate. The petition establishes the applicant's bona fides, readiness to cooperate with proceedings, and overseas ties. If granted, the court directs that in the event of arrest (including at immigration upon landing in India), the applicant shall be released on bail subject to specified conditions and local sureties.

A Lookout Circular (LOC) is an immigration alert issued through the Ministry of Home Affairs and Bureau of Immigration at the request of investigating agencies to monitor or restrict the international movement of an individual facing criminal proceedings. An arbitrary, unjustified, or disproportionate LOC can be challenged by filing a Writ Petition under Article 226 of the Constitution of India before the High Court, seeking judicial suspension or quashing of the LOC and permission to travel abroad.

Under Section 10(3)(e) and 10(3)(h) of the Passports Act, 1967, regional passport authorities or Indian consulates abroad may withhold passport renewal if criminal proceedings are pending before an Indian magistrate court. Under Ministry of External Affairs Notification GSR 570(E), an applicant can file an application before the trial court seized of the matter seeking explicit directions permitting passport renewal, which courts routinely grant for periods ranging from 1 to 3 years.

When an overseas accused does not appear pursuant to summons or warrants, trial courts may initiate proclamation proceedings under Section 84 of the BNSS (earlier Section 82 CrPC) and subsequent property attachment under Section 85 BNSS (earlier Section 83 CrPC). An advocate can enter appearance, file applications to recall non-bailable warrants (NBWs), demonstrate that summons was not served through prescribed overseas channels, and seek stay of attachment to protect ancestral and acquired properties.

For routine trial dates, an accused represented by counsel can seek exemption from personal attendance under Section 228 BNSS (earlier Section 205 CrPC) or Section 355 BNSS (earlier Section 317 CrPC). Additionally, under Section 356 of the BNSS, if a person declared as a proclaimed offender absconds to evade trial, courts possess statutory jurisdiction to proceed with the trial and record evidence in their absence, making active legal representation essential.

The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), and Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) came into force on 1 July 2024. Under Section 531(2)(a) of the BNSS and Section 358 of the BNS, all investigations, inquiries, trials, and appeals pending prior to 1 July 2024 continue under the erstwhile Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 and Indian Penal Code, 1860. New offences alleged on or after 1 July 2024 are registered and prosecuted under the BNS and BNSS.
