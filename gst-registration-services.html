Statutory Foundation

India's Dual GST Regime & Mandatory Legal Triggers

The Goods and Services Tax (GST) framework in India operates under a dual structural mechanism comprising the Central Goods and Services Tax (CGST) Act, 2017, corresponding State GST (SGST) Acts, and the Integrated Goods and Services Tax (IGST) Act, 2017. Every enterprise, foreign branch, or professional entity engaging in commercial supply must determine statutory liability with absolute legal precision.

While the general statutory threshold stands at INR 40 Lakhs aggregate turnover for goods suppliers (INR 20 Lakhs in Special Category States) and INR 20 Lakhs for service providers (INR 10 Lakhs in Special Category States), Section 24 of the CGST Act mandates compulsory registration irrespective of turnover across key commercial categories.

Inter-State Supplies

Mandatory registration under Sec 24(i) prior to shipping goods across state boundaries.

Non-Resident Persons

Overseas entities supplying in India must register under Sec 24(v) 5 days prior to operations.

E-Commerce Operators

Mandatory GSTIN under Sec 24(x) for digital platform sellers & market operators.

Reverse Charge (RCM)

Compulsory filing under Sec 24(iii) for entities liable to pay tax under reverse charge.

Procedural Roadmap

The Legal GSTIN Registration Lifecycle

A structured, attorney-supervised compliance roadmap from initial jurisdictional audit through final GSTIN issuance and return readiness.

Step 01

Jurisdiction & Tax Audit

Legal assessment of turnover, HSN/SAC codes, Central vs State circle jurisdiction, and evaluation of mandatory Sec 24 triggers or Composition eligibility.

Step 02

Documentation & DSC Setup

Vetting registered lease agreements, title deeds, owner NOCs, partner/director resolutions, and Class 3 Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) cryptographic linkage.

Step 03

Part-A Filing & TRN

Electronic filing of PAN, official email, and mobile OTP authentication on the GST Common Portal to generate the Temporary Reference Number (TRN).

Step 04

Part-B Application (REG-01)

Submission of comprehensive Form GST REG-01 / REG-09 incorporating geo-tagged premises coordinates, promoter KYC, bank mandate, and HSN goods classifications.

Step 05

Clarification & REG-04 Response

In case of officer scrutiny or Form GST REG-03 queries, our advocates prepare and submit robust legal counter-affidavits in Form GST REG-04 within 7 working days.

Step 06

GSTIN Grant & Compliance Handover

Issuance of Certificate of Registration (Form GST REG-06) featuring 15-digit GSTIN, followed by bank account verification and export LUT filing.

Checklist & Verification

Required Documentation for GST Registration

Ensure all documentation complies with statutory file format limits (PDF/JPEG < 100KB-1MB) and contains verified address alignment across municipal and revenue records.

Entity & Promoter KYC

  • Permanent Account Number (PAN) of Entity / Proprietor
  • Aadhaar Card of Proprietor, Partners, or Managing Directors
  • Passport-size photographs (White background, <100KB)
  • Certificate of Incorporation (MCA) / Registered Partnership Deed
  • Memorandum & Articles of Association (MoA & AoA) / LLP Agreement
  • Board Resolution / Letter of Authorization for Authorized Signatory

Principal Place of Business

  • Registered or Notarized Rent / Lease Agreement
  • Latest Electricity Bill / Water Bill / Property Tax Receipt (<2 months)
  • Signed No Objection Certificate (NOC) from property owner
  • Title Deed / Patta Copy for self-owned commercial property
  • Clear geo-tagged photographs of office exterior and entrance board
  • Additional place of business proof (for warehouses, branch godowns)

Banking & Cross-Border

  • Cancelled Cheque bearing Entity Name, Account No, & IFSC
  • First page of Bank Passbook or certified Bank Statement
  • Class 3 Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) for Companies & LLPs
  • Overseas Tax Identification / Business Registration (for NRTP)
  • Apostilled / Consularized Power of Attorney for Indian Signatory
  • Advance Tax Deposit Challan (compulsory for NRTP under Sec 27)

Non-Compliance Penalties & Risks

Mandatory 100% Tax Penalty (Sec 122)

Carrying on business without mandatory registration incurs a penalty of 100% of the tax due or ₹10,000, whichever is higher.

Loss of Input Tax Credit on Stock

Delays past 30 days of crossing liability thresholds permanently forfeit statutory right to claim input credit on existing inventory.

Goods Confiscation & Prosecution (Sec 130/132)

Unauthorized goods transit without valid GST invoice and e-way bill risks full asset confiscation and criminal prosecution for tax evasion.

Indian Resident Signatory

Under Section 24(v) of the CGST Act, every overseas entity registering as an NRTP must appoint an Authorized Signatory residing in India holding a valid Indian PAN. Our chambers provides verified fiduciary representation and documentation drafting.

Advance Tax Deposit (Sec 27)

An NRTP registration certificate is valid for 90 days (extendable up to an additional 90 days). The law mandates depositing estimated tax liability in advance into the government cash ledger prior to certificate grant.

NRI Real Estate Rental GST

NRIs leasing commercial properties in Tamil Nadu with gross rentals exceeding ₹20 Lakhs must register. We manage return filings (GSTR-1, GSTR-3B), tax deductions, and reconciliation with Income Tax Form 26AS/AIS.

Frequently Asked Questions: GST Law & Compliance

Authoritative legal insights into statutory registration triggers, non-resident procedures, and compliance governance in India.

Under the Central Goods and Services Tax (CGST) Act 2017, suppliers of goods must register if aggregate domestic turnover exceeds INR 40 Lakhs (INR 20 Lakhs in Special Category States). For service providers, the mandatory threshold is INR 20 Lakhs (INR 10 Lakhs in Special Category States). However, mandatory registration applies irrespective of turnover under Section 24 for inter-state taxable supplies, casual taxable persons, non-resident taxable persons, e-commerce operators, and persons liable under reverse charge (RCM).

Under Section 24(v) read with Section 27 of the CGST Act, a Non-Resident Taxable Person (NRTP) who occasionally undertakes transactions involving the supply of goods or services but has no fixed place of business or residence in India must apply for GST registration at least 5 days prior to commencing business using Form GST REG-09. An NRTP must appoint an Authorized Signatory who is a resident of India holding a valid Indian PAN, and submit an advance deposit of tax equivalent to the estimated tax liability for the 90-day registration period.

Under Section 16 of the Integrated Goods and Services Tax (IGST) Act 2017, export of goods and services is classified as a 'zero-rated supply.' Exporters can export without paying IGST by furnishing a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) in Form GST RFD-11 annually on the GST Portal, preserving working capital liquidity while remaining eligible to claim accumulated Input Tax Credit (ITC) refunds under Section 54.

For rented or leased premises, the applicant must provide a registered or notarized Lease/Rental Agreement along with an electricity bill, property tax receipt, or water bill in the landlord's name (not older than 2 months), coupled with a signed No Objection Certificate (NOC) in the prescribed format. For owned property, a title deed, municipal tax receipt, or electricity bill bearing the applicant's name is required.

If the GST Tax Officer raises queries regarding address verification, jurisdiction, nature of business, or documentation discrepancies, a Show Cause Notice in Form GST REG-03 is issued. Our legal team drafts a formal statutory response in Form GST REG-04 within the mandatory 7 working days, submitting authenticating title deeds, geo-tagged photographs, lease documents, and board resolutions to ensure prompt GSTIN approval without rejection.

Yes. Commercial leasing of immovable property situated in India is deemed a supply of service. If an NRI's aggregate rental income from commercial properties exceeds INR 20 Lakhs annually, or if leasing to registered business entities under specific contractual terms, GST registration is mandatory. Our firm facilitates local authorized signatory appointment, PAN alignment, and regular return compliance.

Under Section 122(1)(xi) of the CGST Act, failure to obtain registration carries a penalty of 100% of the tax due or INR 10,000, whichever is higher. Continued intentional evasion can trigger asset attachment under Section 83, confiscation of goods in transit under Section 130, and criminal prosecution under Section 132 for substantial tax liabilities.

Under Section 10 of the CGST Act, small domestic businesses with turnover up to INR 1.5 Crores (INR 50 Lakhs for service providers) may opt for the Composition Scheme to pay tax at a nominal flat rate (1% to 6%) with simplified quarterly returns. However, composite dealers cannot issue tax invoices, cannot collect GST from customers, cannot claim Input Tax Credit, and cannot engage in inter-state outward supplies. Regular registration allows full ITC claims and unhindered national trade.
