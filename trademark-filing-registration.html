Strategic Asset Defense

Why Formal Trademark Protection Is Indispensable

In commercial trade, your brand name, logo, packaging, and slogan represent your single most valuable intangible commercial asset. Operating with an unregistered mark exposes your business to catastrophic brand dilution, opportunistic trademark squatting, and expensive passing-off litigation.

Governed by the Trade Marks Act, 1999 and the Trade Marks Rules, 2017, obtaining a formal registration certificate grants exclusive statutory rights across all states of India, empowers civil and criminal remedies against counterfeiters, and creates a licensable asset eligible for commercial franchising and investment valuation.

Eligibility Framework

Who Can File a Trademark Application?

Any person, corporate entity, or legal association claiming bona fide ownership or prior commercial usage in India is eligible.

Individuals & Proprietors

Individual entrepreneurs, solo inventors, and proprietorship businesses operating under personal names or trade names.

Standard Govt Fee: ₹4,500

MSMEs & Startups

Enterprises possessing a valid Udyam Registration or DPIIT recognition certificate enjoy a 50% government fee concession.

Concessional Fee: ₹4,500

Corporate Entities

Private Limited companies, Public Limited entities, LLPs, partnerships, and Section 8 non-profits requiring institutional brand protection.

Standard Govt Fee: ₹9,000

Foreign & NRI Applicants

Overseas citizens, foreign corporations, and NRI businesses filing directly in India with an address for service in India.

Statutory Foundation

Statutory Governance Under Indian Law

Trademark law in India is anchored in the Trade Marks Act, 1999, which aligns Indian trademark jurisprudence with the TRIPS Agreement and international standards under the Paris Convention.

The administration of trademark registries is governed by the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks (CGPDTM). For applicants residing or having their principal place of business in Tamil Nadu, the jurisdictional Trade Marks Registry is situated in Chennai.

  • Section 28: Exclusive right to the use of the trademark in relation to the goods or services for which it is registered.
  • Section 29: Statutory grounds defining trademark infringement by unauthorized third parties.
  • Section 135: Judicial relief available in infringement and passing off suits, including injunctions, damages, and delivery up.

Unregistered vs Registered Rights

Under common law, unregistered marks rely solely on the tort of passing off under Section 27(2), which requires extensive, burdensome proof of goodwill, misrepresentation, and actual financial damage.

By contrast, a registered mark establishes prima facie validity under Section 31. In an infringement suit, the registered proprietor does not need to prove consumer confusion or commercial goodwill—the unauthorized identical or deceptive use itself creates liability.

Cautionary Note: Government filing does not guarantee automatic registration. Absolute or relative objections must be resolved professionally.

Territorial Scope Notice

Please note that trademark rights are strictly territorial. Registering a trademark in India protects your brand exclusively within the physical boundaries of the Republic of India.

If you sell goods or provide software services in the United States, United Kingdom, European Union, or UAE, separate national or Madrid Protocol applications must be initiated in those specific target jurisdictions. Our IP desk coordinates the necessary overseas priority filings.

The ™ symbol indicates that a trademark application has been filed and an application number has been allotted by the Trade Marks Registry. It puts competitors on notice of your claimed rights. The ® symbol can strictly only be used once the Registrar formally issues the Certificate of Registration under Section 23 of the Trade Marks Act, 1999. Falsely representing an unregistered mark with ® is a punishable statutory offence under Section 107.

Under normal circumstances without third-party opposition, the trademark registration process typically concludes in 6 to 12 months. This duration encompasses formal examination by the registry (1 to 2 months), objection reply drafting (30 days if required), statutory publication in the Trade Marks Journal (4-month opposition period), and final certificate generation.

Section 9 objections deal with absolute grounds for refusal—specifically that the mark is generic, laudatory, or directly describes the quality/character of the goods or services. Section 11 objections deal with relative grounds for refusal—namely that the mark is identical or confusingly similar to an existing earlier mark on the register. Both require formal, evidence-backed legal replies filed within 30 days of receiving the Examination Report.

The Nice Classification classifies goods in Classes 1–34 and services in Classes 35–45. You should register in all classes where your company currently operates or has concrete business plans to expand. While multi-class applications under Form TM-A are permissible, government filing fees are calculated per class (₹4,500 for individuals/startups/MSMEs and ₹9,000 for standard corporate entities).

A registered trademark in India is valid for 10 years from the application filing date. It can be renewed indefinitely every 10 years by filing Form TM-R along with the statutory renewal fee within 6 months prior to its expiry date.
