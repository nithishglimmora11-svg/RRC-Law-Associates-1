Customs Valuation Disputes and Related-Party Imports

Transaction Value under Section 14, Special Valuation Branch (SVB) Procedures, Transfer Pricing Synergy, and CVR 2007

14 min read
Reviewed by RRC Customs Litigation Practice
Updated November 2026

Key Takeaways & At a Glance

  • Under Section 14(1) of the Customs Act, 1962, the 'Transaction Value'—the price actually paid or payable for goods when sold for export to India—is the primary and sacrosanct basis of customs valuation.
  • Transactions between 'related persons' (holding/subsidiary, joint ventures, common directors, 5%+ voting shares) are subject to Special Valuation Branch (SVB) scrutiny under CBIC Circulars 04/2016 and 05/2016.
  • Being related does NOT automatically invalidate the declared transaction value. Under Rule 3(3) of the Customs Valuation Rules (CVR), 2007, the declared price MUST be accepted if the relationship did not influence the price.
  • Importers can establish that the price was not influenced by presenting 'Test Values' (identical/similar sales to independent Indian buyers) or by showing that the price covers all costs plus a normal commercial profit.
  • Transfer pricing studies prepared for income tax purposes provide valuable supporting evidence, but customs authorities require specific customs-centric valuation benchmarking under CVR 2007.

Understanding the Legal Issue

In modern multinational commerce, an overwhelming volume of international trade occurs between corporate affiliates: foreign parent corporations exporting capital machinery, raw materials, or finished products to their Indian subsidiaries, joint ventures, or regional distribution hubs. Under Indian customs law, transactions between related commercial entities are viewed with acute regulatory scrutiny to ensure that import values are not artificially depressed to minimize ad-valorem customs duties.

The legal mechanism governing these cross-border affiliate transactions is administered through the Special Valuation Branch (SVB) under Section 14 of the Customs Act, 1962, and the Customs Valuation (Determination of Value of Imported Goods) Rules, 2007 (CVR 2007). Merely having common shareholding or corporate affiliation does not justify arbitrary valuation upliftment by the department. Understanding how to navigate SVB questionnaires (Annexure A), defend transaction value under Rule 3(3), demonstrate that the relationship has not influenced pricing, and align customs valuation with income tax transfer pricing documentation is vital to maintaining smooth import logistics.

Key Provisions of the Customs Act, 1962

Customs proceedings in India are governed strictly by codified parliamentary statutes and binding notifications issued by the Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC). The foundational statutory provisions applicable to this matter include:

Section 14(1) Customs Act, 1962

Prescribes Transaction Value as the foundational basis of customs valuation, requiring buyers and sellers to be unrelated, or if related, that the price is not influenced.

Rule 2(2), CVR 2007 Customs Valuation Rules

Statutorily defines 'related persons', including officers/directors, partners, employer-employee, holding-subsidiary, and 5%+ shareholding.

Rule 3(3), CVR 2007 Customs Valuation Rules

Mandates acceptance of transaction value in related party imports if circumstances of sale demonstrate arm's length pricing or match test values.

CBIC Circulars 04 & 05/2016 CBIC Guidelines

Overhauled SVB procedures, abolishing automatic Extra Duty Deposit (EDD) and establishing standardized investigation procedures.

In-Depth Legal Analysis & Practical Application

1. Who is a 'Related Person' under Customs Law?

Rule 2(2) of CVR 2007 enumerates eight specific relational tests:

  • They are officers or directors of one another's businesses.
  • They are legally recognized partners in business.
  • An employer and employee relationship exists.
  • Any person directly or indirectly owns, controls, or holds 5% or more of the outstanding voting stock or shares in both.
  • One directly or indirectly controls the other (e.g., holding-subsidiary).
  • Both are directly or indirectly controlled by a third person.
  • Together they directly or indirectly control a third person.
  • They are members of the same family.

2. The Two Golden Tests under Rule 3(3)

When parties are related, the declared value can be defended through two independent statutory routes:

1. Circumstances of Sale Test (Rule 3(3)(a)): Demonstrating that the overseas seller settles prices with the Indian buyer in a manner consistent with normal commercial pricing practices of the industry, covering total costs plus representative profit.

2. Test Values Method (Rule 3(3)(b)): Demonstrating that the declared transaction value closely approximates:

  • The transaction value of identical goods sold to unrelated buyers in India.
  • The deductive value under Rule 7 or computed value under Rule 8 of identical/similar goods.

3. The SVB Modern Procedure (Circulars 04/2016 & 05/2016)

Prior to 2016, SVB investigations imposed severe burdens through indefinite provisional assessments and compulsory 1% to 5% Extra Duty Deposits (EDD). Under current CBIC guidelines:

  • No Automatic EDD: Extra Duty Deposit is completely abolished for routine SVB inquiries.
  • Annexure A Scrutiny: The importer submits a detailed questionnaire (Annexure A) with supply agreements, royalty contracts, and transfer pricing documentation at the port of import.
  • Centralized SVB Referral: If full investigation is warranted, files are referred to dedicated SVB cells (e.g., Chennai, Mumbai, Delhi), which are mandated to finalize investigations and issue an advisory report within a stipulated timeframe.

Step-by-Step Legal Procedure

Navigating customs defense requires strict adherence to statutory sequences. The standard operational procedure followed by experienced customs litigation counsel includes:

Special Valuation Branch (SVB) Investigation & Final Valuation Procedure

Declare related-party relationship under Rule 2(2) of Customs Valuation Rules, 2007, on Bill of Entry upon arrival.

2

SVB Questionnaire Filing

Submit Annexure-A questionnaire within 30 days of import to SVB along with audited transfer pricing TP study.

3

Arm's Length Demonstration

Demonstrate under Rule 3(3) that transaction price closely approximates value of identical/similar goods imported by unrelated parties.

Investigation Report & Finalization

Obtain SVB Investigation Report (IR) accepting declared value without Extra Duty Deposit (EDD); finalize provisional assessments.

Essential Documents & Evidence Checklist

To substantiate claims, establish commercial bonafides, and refute departmental allegations, assemble the following documentary evidence:

  • Completed SVB Questionnaire (Annexure A) with corporate verification
  • Intercompany Master Supply Agreement, Distribution Agreement, or Technology Licensing Contract
  • Annual Audited Financial Statements of Indian entity and foreign supplier
  • Transfer Pricing Study Report prepared for Indian Income Tax purposes
  • Price lists for Indian market vs. third-country independent markets
  • Invoices showing identical/similar goods sold to independent Indian buyers (if available)

Practical Case Scenario

Scenario: A German automotive component giant established a 100% subsidiary in Sriperumbudur, Tamil Nadu, to assemble transmission systems. The Indian subsidiary imported sub-assemblies from its German parent. Customs Appraising Group referred the imports to the Chennai SVB, proposing to uplift the import transaction value by 22% by adding global research & development (R&D) and trademark royalties under Rule 10(1)(c) of CVR 2007, demanding ₹8.5 Crores in differential duty.

Legal Resolution: RRC Law Associates examined the intercompany technical collaboration agreement. We established that the R&D payments were related exclusively to post-import vehicle integration services conducted within India, not as a condition of sale for the imported sub-assemblies. Furthermore, we conducted a cost-plus benchmark under Rule 3(3)(a) proving that the German parent sold identical sub-assemblies to unrelated assemblers in Japan and Mexico at identical pricing. The Chennai SVB accepted our submissions in full, issuing an order accepting the declared transaction value without any upliftment or royalty additions.

Common Legal Pitfalls to Avoid

Critical Procedural Mistakes

  • Failing to declare related-party status in the Bill of Entry, which exposes the company to severe fraud penalties under Section 114A.
  • Relying solely on Income Tax Transfer Pricing reports without conducting a specific customs valuation analysis under CVR 2007.
  • Agreeing to arbitrary valuation loading under SVB without demanding a speaking, appealable order under Section 17(5).
  • Overlooking Rule 10 additions, particularly assists, engineering drawings, and patent royalties paid to overseas affiliates.

The SVB is a specialized wing of Indian Customs tasked with examining the influence of relationships on import pricing, as well as complex royalty, license fee, and technical assistance payments under Rule 10.

Under CBIC Circular 05/2016, automatic Extra Duty Deposit (EDD) has been abolished. Importers who provide required documents and Annexure A information within statutory deadlines are not required to pay EDD.

While income tax transfer pricing and customs valuation share the common goal of arm's length pricing, they operate under distinct statutory rules. Customs is not legally bound by an income tax assessment, but a well-structured transfer pricing study provides persuasive evidentiary support.

Conclusion & Strategic Advice

Customs law disputes in India require swift, technically informed, and legally verified advocacy. Because customs authorities operate under stringent statutory limitation deadlines—from the 30-day Show Cause Notice window to the strict 60-day appellate period under Section 128—delay or informal verbal negotiations frequently compromise substantive legal rights.

Engaging specialized customs legal counsel at the earliest stage of search, summons, seizure, or notice issuance ensures that your business preserves documentary evidence, maintains procedural compliance, and enforces all statutory protections guaranteed under Indian law.
