Statutory Foundation

DPIIT Gazette Notification G.S.R. 127(E) & Startup Governance

The legal definition of an Indian startup is codified by the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT) under Gazette Notification G.S.R. 127(E). This framework provides an institutional bridge between early-stage innovation ventures and transformative corporate tax holidays, intellectual property subsidies, and public procurement relaxations.

Navigating DPIIT recognition and Inter-Ministerial Board (IMB) certification requires rigorous legal drafting to articulate technological uniqueness, defensible intellectual property, and high employment or wealth generation potential.

10-Year Horizon

Retain statutory startup classification for up to 10 years from the date of incorporation.

₹100 Crore Ceiling

Maintain benefits until annual turnover exceeds ₹100 Crores in any financial year.

Statutory Benchmark Summary

Core eligibility benchmarks mandated by DPIIT Gazette Notifications:

Statutory Requirement Legal Standard
Entity Structure Pvt Ltd / LLP / Regd Partnership
Maximum Company Age Up to 10 Years from Incorporation
Turnover Ceiling Not exceeding ₹100 Crores in any FY
Nature of Business Innovation, Development, or Scalability
Splitting / Reconstruction Strictly Disqualified
Sole proprietorships and un-registered entities are legally barred from DPIIT recognition until converted into an eligible corporate structure.

Legal Clarity

Frequently Asked Questions: Startup India & DPIIT Recognition

Authoritative legal insights into statutory eligibility, Section 80-IAC tax holidays, angel tax relief, and IP rebates.

Under DPIIT Notification G.S.R. 127(E), an entity qualifies as a 'Startup' if: (1) It is incorporated as a Private Limited Company, Registered Partnership Firm, or Limited Liability Partnership (LLP); (2) Period of existence does not exceed 10 years from incorporation; (3) Turnover in any financial year since incorporation does not exceed INR 100 Crores; (4) It works towards innovation, development, or improvement of products, processes, or services, or has a scalable business model with high potential of employment generation or wealth creation; and (5) It is not formed by splitting up or reconstructing an existing business.

Under Section 80-IAC of the Income Tax Act 1961, an eligible startup incorporated between 1st April 2016 and 31st March 2025 (or as extended by Finance Acts) can claim a 100% tax deduction on business profits for three consecutive assessment years out of a block of ten years. This tax holiday requires a separate certification from the Inter-Ministerial Board (IMB) of Certification, requiring a detailed dossier demonstrating technological innovation and economic scalability.

Section 56(2)(viib) taxes share premium received by closely held companies above Fair Market Value (FMV) as income from other sources ('Angel Tax'). DPIIT-recognized startups that file Form 2 with the Department are completely exempt from Section 56(2)(viib), provided aggregate paid-up capital and share premium after issuance does not exceed INR 25 Crores (excluding investments from non-residents, venture capital funds, and specified listed companies).

Under the Scheme for Facilitating Startups Intellectual Property Protection (SIPP) and amended Patent Rules 2003, DPIIT startups enjoy: (1) 80% rebate in official patent filing fees; (2) 50% rebate in trademark application fees; (3) Fast-track expedited patent examination under Rule 24B(1)(b), reducing patent grant times from 4-5 years down to under 12 months; and (4) Panel of government-empaneled patent/trademark facilitators whose professional drafting fees are reimbursed by the Government.

Yes. An Indian Private Limited Company or LLP founded by NRIs or foreign investors is fully eligible for DPIIT recognition. Furthermore, recognized startups are permitted to issue Convertible Notes to persons resident outside India under Regulation 6A of FEMA 20(R) for amounts of INR 25 Lakhs or more in a single tranche, convertible into equity shares within 10 years.

Recognized startups are exempt from the criteria of 'prior turnover' and 'prior experience' in public procurement tenders floated by Central Ministries, Departments, and Public Sector Undertakings (CPSUs), subject to meeting technical capability parameters. Startups are also 100% exempt from depositing Earnest Money Deposit (EMD) or bid security.

DPIIT-recognized startups are permitted to self-certify compliance through a simple online return under 6 core Central Labour Laws (including EPF, ESI, Payment of Gratuity, and Contract Labour) and 3 Environmental Laws (Water, Air, and Environmental Protection Acts) for a period of up to 5 years from incorporation, with no inspection conducted without prior written permission from a high-ranking officer.

Under Section 55 to 58 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, DPIIT-recognized startups qualify as 'fast-track corporate insolvency resolution entities.' In the event of business closure, a recognized startup can voluntarily wind up and liquidate its operations within 90 days (compared to the standard 180 days for other commercial companies).
