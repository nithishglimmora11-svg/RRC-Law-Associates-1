Tax Implications When NRIs Sell Property in India

A comprehensive legal and direct tax analysis: long-term vs. short-term capital gains classification, Section 195 withholding burdens, Section 197 lower TDS certificates (Form 13), Section 54/54EC exemptions, Section 50C circle rate benchmarks, and RBI FEMA outward repatriation protocols.

Authored by RRC Law Associates
Published: October 2026
15 min read
Verified for 2026 Compliance
Comprehensive legal and tax coordination for non-residents selling real estate, securing lower TDS certificates, and remitting proceeds abroad.

For non-resident Indians and OCI cardholders, selling real estate in India—whether ancestral agricultural land, residential villas, or commercial buildings—represents one of the most high-value transactions they will ever undertake. It is also the area where overseas sellers encounter the most stringent regulatory friction.

Unlike resident property sales where buyers simply deduct 1% TDS, sales involving non-resident owners trigger automatic, heavy withholding tax under Section 195 (ranging from 20.8% to 23.92% on gross consideration), stringent Section 50C circle rate checks, and strict Reserve Bank of India (RBI) Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA) documentation before a single dollar or pound can leave the country.

1. Classification of Capital Gains: Short-Term vs. Long-Term Real Estate Assets

When an immovable property (land, building, or apartment) situated in India is transferred, the resulting profit is classified under Section 45 as Capital Gains. The tax rate is determined by the statutory holding period:

  • Long-Term Capital Asset (LTCA): Immovable property held for more than 24 months immediately preceding the date of transfer. Gains are classified as Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG).
  • Short-Term Capital Asset (STCA): Immovable property held for 24 months or less. Gains are classified as Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) and added to the non-resident's total income, taxed at normal individual slab rates.
Statutory Direct Tax Amendments

Under revised direct tax provisions (transitioning into the Income-tax Act, 2025 framework), long-term capital gains on immovable property are taxed at a rationalized baseline rate of 12.5% without indexation for assets acquired on or after 23 July 2024.

For properties acquired by an individual prior to 23 July 2024, an elective grandfathering mechanism permits the taxpayer to compute liability at either 12.5% without indexation or 20% with Cost Inflation Index (CII) indexation, ensuring that non-residents who held real estate over decades are protected against arbitrary nominal gains.

2. Inherited & Ancestral Property: Determining Cost of Acquisition & Holding Period

A substantial percentage of properties sold by NRIs across Tamil Nadu (Chennai, Thanjavur, Madurai, Coimbatore) and across India are acquired by inheritance or registered gift from deceased parents or ancestors.

Calculating capital gains on inherited real estate is governed by specific statutory provisions:

  1. Zero Inheritance Tax: The transmission of property through a registered Will, legal heirship certificate, or succession partition is completely exempt from tax at the time of transfer.
  2. Cost to Previous Owner (Section 49(1)): When the NRI ultimately sells the property, the "Cost of Acquisition" is legally deemed to be the cost for which the last previous owner purchased it.
  3. Holding Period Inclusivity: Under Section 2(42A), the holding period of the previous owner is added to the holding period of the NRI. Consequently, virtually all inherited properties qualify as Long-Term Capital Assets, even if the NRI inherited the title just months prior to sale.
  4. Properties Acquired Before 1 April 2001: If the original ancestor acquired the asset prior to 1 April 2001, the taxpayer is entitled under Section 55 to substitute the actual purchase cost with the Fair Market Value (FMV) as of 1 April 2001 (supported by a Government Registered Valuer's report), capped at the property's stamp duty guideline circle value as of that date.

3. Mandatory Section 195 TDS on Property Sale: The 20% + Surcharge Burden

The single greatest shock to non-resident sellers is Section 195 withholding. Unlike sales between Indian residents where the buyer deducts a modest 1% TDS under Section 194-IA, a sale by an NRI triggers mandatory Section 195 withholding at 20% plus surcharge and 4% cess.

Sale Consideration Value Base TDS Rate Surcharge Rate Effective TDS Withheld
Up to INR 50 Lakhs 20.00% 0% 20.80%
INR 50 Lakhs to 1 Crore 20.00% 10% 22.88%
INR 1 Crore to 2 Crores 20.00% 15% 23.92%
Above INR 2 Crores 20.00% Capped Surcharge 23.92%
The Total Consideration Trap

Under statutory rules, unless a specific certificate is obtained, the buyer is required to deduct 20.8%–23.92% on the total gross sale consideration, not just the capital gains profit!

For example, on a property sold for INR 2 Crores where the actual capital gain is only INR 30 Lakhs, the buyer will withhold nearly INR 48 Lakhs in tax—locking away more than the entire profit for over a year until an ITR is filed and processed.

4. Lower or Nil TDS Deduction: Applying for a Section 197 Certificate (Form 13)

To prevent liquidity lockup, Section 197 of the Act provides a powerful legal remedy: the Lower or Nil TDS Certificate.

By submitting an online Form 13 application on the TRACES portal before executing the registered sale deed, the NRI presents their complete computation: original purchase cost, FMV valuation, improvement expenses, and intended capital gains reinvestments under Section 54 or 54EC.

  • Jurisdictional Assessing Officer (AO): The International Taxation circle reviews the deed copies, valuation certificates, and seller's tax records.
  • Issuance of Certificate: The AO issues a statutory certificate directing the buyer to deduct TDS only on the actual computed capital gain, or at a specific reduced rate (frequently between 0% and 5%).
  • Processing Timeline: The process typically requires 30 to 45 business days. Engaging counsel to initiate the Form 13 application immediately upon signing the Agreement to Sell is critical to avoid closing delays.

5. Statutory Exemptions to Mitigate Capital Gains: Section 54, 54EC & 54F

Non-resident Indians can legally eliminate or significantly reduce their capital gains liability by utilizing statutory rollover exemptions under the Act:

Section 54: Reinvestment in Residential Property

If an NRI sells a residential property held for more than 24 months, the capital gains are completely exempt from tax if the gains are reinvested in purchasing one residential property in India within 1 year before or 2 years after the sale date, or constructing one within 3 years. (Under budget rationalization, a statutory cap of INR 10 Crores applies).

Section 54EC: Capital Gains Bonds

For sellers who do not wish to purchase more real estate in India, Section 54EC permits exemption by investing the capital gains in specified redeemable bonds issued by:

  • Rural Electrification Corporation (REC)
  • Power Finance Corporation (PFC)
  • National Highways Authority of India (NHAI)

Conditions: The investment must be executed within 6 months from the date of the sale deed, is subject to a statutory lock-in period of 5 years, and is capped at a maximum of INR 50 Lakhs per financial year.

Capital Gains Account Scheme (CGAS), 1988

If the capital gain has not been reinvested prior to the due date of filing the ITR (July 31), the unutilized funds must be deposited into a designated CGAS account with an authorized public sector bank before filing to preserve the exemption claim.

6. The Stamp Duty Value Benchmark (Section 50C) & Circle Rate Rules

Under Section 50C of the Act, if an immovable property is transferred for a consideration lower than the value adopted or assessed by the State Government Stamp Valuation Authority (commonly referred to as the Circle Rate, Ready Reckoner Rate, or Guideline Value), the stamp duty value is legally deemed to be the full value of consideration for computing the seller's capital gains.

Safe Harbor Tolerance: If the difference between the contractual sale price and the government guideline value does not exceed 10%, the contractual consideration is accepted.

If the guideline value exceeds the contract price by more than 10%, not only is the seller taxed on the higher fictitious amount, but the resident or non-resident buyer is also taxed under Section 56(2)(x) on the difference as "Income from Other Sources." If you dispute the valuation due to legal encumbrances, waterlogging, or tenancy issues, you must formally petition the Assessing Officer to refer the matter to the District Valuation Officer (DVO).

7. Repatriation of Property Sale Proceeds Abroad: RBI FEMA Regulations

Under RBI Master Direction on Remittance of Assets, the legal framework for moving property sale proceeds abroad from an NRO account operates under clear parameters:

  1. The USD 1 Million Facility: An NRI or OCI cardholder is permitted to remit up to USD 1,000,000 (One Million US Dollars) per financial year (April to March) out of balances held in their NRO account representing legitimate property sale proceeds.
  2. Property Acquired from Foreign Exchange (Inward Remittance): If the residential property was originally purchased out of inward remittances via NRE account or foreign exchange banking channels, proceeds from the sale of up to two residential properties can be remitted freely under specific FEMA general permissions.
  3. Inherited Real Estate: Sale proceeds of inherited properties can be remitted within the USD 1 Million ceiling without restriction on the number of properties, provided clear proof of inheritance (Will, legal heirship certificate, partition deed) is furnished to the Authorized Dealer Bank.

8. Procedural Remittance Protocol: Form 15CA & Form 15CB Certification

To execute the outward telegraphic wire transfer, the Authorized Dealer (AD) Category-I bank requires statutory tax clearance:

Bank Remittance Filing Package
  • Form 15CB (CA Certificate): A practicing Indian Chartered Accountant audits the source of funds, original purchase deed, sale deed, Section 197 certificate or TDS payment challans, and computes the exact tax compliance.
  • Form 15CA (Taxpayer Undertaking): Filed electronically on the official income tax portal under Part C using the unique acknowledgment number from Form 15CB.
  • FEMA Form A2: Outward remittance application form submitted to the remitting bank.
  • NRO Bank Statement: Verifying credit of sale proceeds via official banking channels (RTGS/NEFT/Cheque) matching the registered deed consideration.

9. Double Taxation Avoidance & Claiming Foreign Tax Credit (FTC) Overseas

Under international tax law and bilateral Double Taxation Avoidance Agreements (DTAAs), the country in which immovable property is situated holds the primary right of taxation (Article 6 and Article 13 of OECD/UN model conventions).

However, if the NRI is a tax resident of a country that taxes worldwide income (such as the United States or United Kingdom), the capital gain must also be reported in their domestic foreign tax return.

To prevent paying tax twice, the seller can claim Foreign Tax Credit (FTC):

  • United States: File IRS Form 1116 (Foreign Tax Credit) attached to Form 1040, offsetting Indian tax against US federal liability.
  • United Kingdom: Claim Foreign Tax Credit Relief (FTCR) in the Foreign pages (SA106) of the HMRC Self Assessment return.
  • Canada: Claim Federal Foreign Tax Credit via CRA Form T2209.

10. Practical Legal Checklist Before Signing the Sale Deed

Pre-Closing Conveyancing Checklist
  • Title Due Diligence & Encumbrance Certificate (EC): Obtain nil-encumbrance records for at least 30 years from the Sub-Registrar Office (SRO).
  • Special Power of Attorney (SPA): If executing through a trusted relative in India, ensure the SPA is drafted specifically for the property, attested at the Indian Consulate/Embassy abroad, and duly adjudicated/stamped at the local District Collectorate in India.
  • Verify Buyer's TAN Registration: Confirm the buyer holds an active TAN to deposit Section 195 TDS; never allow a buyer to deposit under Form 26QB (resident 1% TDS).
  • Initiate Form 13 Lower TDS Application: Apply at least 30 to 45 days prior to the proposed execution date.
  • Maintain Banking Segregation: Ensure the buyer draws payment strictly in favor of the seller's designated NRO bank account.
